From 1 January 2027, every Indian marketplace has to mark sponsored listings "clearly and prominently" and publish the main parameters that rank products, in descending order, in plain language. The rules were notified on 9 September 2026. For D2C brands, the practical effect is that a paid slot will look like a paid slot, and your organic rank will have to earn its place on stated factors.

If most of your marketplace revenue comes from sponsored placements that shoppers read as organic, this is the rule to model before Q4 budgets are locked.

## What was actually notified

The Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) (Amendment) Rules, 2026 were notified on 9 September 2026 and take effect on 1 January 2027. Several law-firm summaries run the changes together, so it helps to separate the three that touch advertising.

First, sponsored listings must be distinctly identified. Bar & Bench's reading places this in Rule 4(12). Second, marketplaces must explain their principal ranking parameters "in descending order" together with their relative importance (Rule 5(3)(f)). Third, a marketplace may not manipulate search results or search indexes in a way that misleads users about their query (Rule 4(11)(c)).

One caveat on sources. Summaries disagree on small points such as effective dates and penalty figures, and the official gazette text is the one to check before you brief a client. We have only relied on points that at least two independent summaries agree on.

## Where the rule bites for D2C brands

Marketplaces already show a small "Sponsored" or "Ad" tag. The rule changes the standard from "present somewhere" to "clear and prominent", and it makes the marketplace, not you, the party that answers for it. That matters because the platforms will redesign result pages to comply, and the redesign will change click behaviour.

Our expectation, which is a judgement and not a finding: a more visible tag lowers click-through on sponsored slots for categories where shoppers compare on price. It will not change much for categories where the brand name is the search query. Measure it. Do not assume it.

| Item | Before | From 1 January 2027 |
|---|---|---|
| Sponsored slot | Small tag, platform's choice | Distinctly and prominently identified |
| Organic ranking | Opaque | Main parameters listed in descending order |
| Search manipulation | Platform policy only | Prohibited where it misleads the user |
| Consumer data for private labels | Platform policy | "Express and affirmative consent" needed for some uses |

## The ranking disclosure is the quiet one

Rule 5(3)(f) does not ask a marketplace to publish its algorithm. It asks for the main parameters, ranked by importance, with an explanation. That is a different thing, and Bar & Bench is explicit that it is about visibility factors, not source code.

For a D2C team this is useful. Today most of us infer ranking drivers from our own data: conversion rate, rating, delivery promise, price, stock availability. Once the platform publishes its own list, you can stop guessing and start auditing. If "delivery speed" sits above "rating" on the published list, your fulfilment spend may buy more rank than another round of review acquisition.

> A published ranking list is a brief for your merchandising team, not a legal footnote.

## What to change before the rules take effect

Most clients we work with run marketplace ads and brand-site ads from separate dashboards, and only compare them in a monthly deck. That is the first thing to fix, because the label change will shift the split between sponsored and organic orders.

Here is the order we would do it in:

1. Tag your October and November festive data by placement type, sponsored versus organic, so you have a clean baseline before the rules apply.
2. Rerun your blended ROAS with sponsored orders separated. If your organic share was being flattered by shoppers who clicked an unlabelled paid slot, you will see it.
3. When each marketplace publishes its ranking parameters, map them to what you control: price, availability, delivery promise, content quality, ratings.
4. Shift budget toward the parameters at the top of the list, not the ones that are cheapest to move.

## The seller-side duties that land on you directly

Alongside the advertising rules, the amendment adds listing disclosures for sellers. Legal500 and Mondaq both list government identification numbers (a GSTIN or MSME registration number), country of origin, and clear return, refund, exchange, warranty and delivery information. Legal500 adds payment modes and best-before dates where relevant.

None of that is glamorous, but it is where a listing audit pays off. We would sample your top 50 SKUs on each marketplace, check which of these fields are empty or inconsistent with your own site, and fix them in one sprint. Thin listings are also the ones that rank poorly on content quality, so the work does double duty.

## The private-label data restriction

A third provision matters to brands that sell on marketplaces that also sell their own goods. Under Rule 5(6) as summarised by Bar & Bench, marketplaces cannot use consumer information to favour goods bearing the marketplace's own brand name, and need "express and affirmative consent" before using consumer information to promote sellers as associated with the marketplace.

We would not build a strategy on this yet. Enforcement will decide what it means in practice. Still, if you have ever suspected a private label appearing above you for your own branded keywords, document it now with dated screenshots.

## What is not settled

Penalties are the gap. The summaries we read either do not state any or disagree. We would treat the compliance burden for labels and ranking disclosure as sitting with the marketplace, and the seller disclosure duties as the part that lands on you.

Also unsettled: how "prominent" will be judged. Expect platforms to pick the safest design, which probably means a larger tag. Our recommendation is simple. Treat 1 January 2027 as the date your sponsored and organic numbers stop being comparable with last year's, and build the baseline in October, while the old layout is still live.
