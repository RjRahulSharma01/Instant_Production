If you sell, list or cook anything made from vegetable fat that looks like paneer, the word "paneer" is about to become off-limits for it. FSSAI's draft amendment of 22 September 2026 says products registered under "Analogue in Dairy Context" must drop "paneer" from their name, labelling and marketing. The public comment window runs 60 days from the 23 September Gazette date, which puts it around 22 November.

That is the headline. The second half of the story landed a week ago: FSSAI has also notified a mandatory blue-and-white milk-drop logo for genuine dairy packs, enforceable from 1 July 2028. Read together, the two moves tell you exactly where the regulator is heading, and most of the copy work sits with marketing, not the factory.

## What the draft actually says

The instrument is the Food Safety and Standards (Prohibition and Restrictions on Sales) Amendment Regulations, 2026, issued as a draft under Section 92 of the Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006. It inserts a new clause under Regulation 2.1.1 prohibiting the sale of "paneer made of constituents not derived from milk."

The Ministry of Health and Family Welfare framed the aim plainly: to ensure that "only milk-derived products are represented as 'Paneer'." Note the word "represented." That is broader than the front of the pack. It reaches the product name on a quick commerce listing, the dish name on a delivery app, the Reels caption and the search ad headline.

It is still a draft. Objections and suggestions go to the CEO, FSSAI, or to regulation@fssai.gov.in, and FSSAI will consider them after the 60-day window. Final text can change. The direction of travel will not.

## The dairy logo makes the gap visible

The second instrument is final. The Food Safety and Standards (Labelling and Display) Second Amendment Regulations, 2026 were notified on 6 November and published in the Gazette on 9 November. From 1 July 2028, milk, milk products and composite milk products must carry a white milk drop inside a solid blue square on the principal display panel. Brands cannot design their own version.

Minimum sizes scale with the display panel:

| Principal display panel area | Blue square (minimum) | White drop (height x width) |
|---|---|---|
| Up to 100 sq cm | 6 mm x 6 mm | 4.56 mm x 3.00 mm |
| Over 100 to 500 sq cm | 8 mm x 8 mm | 6.08 mm x 4.00 mm |
| Over 500 to 2,500 sq cm | 12 mm x 12 mm | 9.12 mm x 6.00 mm |
| Over 2,500 sq cm | 16 mm x 16 mm | 12.16 mm x 8.00 mm |

Trade coverage has tied the logo to the fight against vegetable-oil analogues sold as paneer, khoya and ghee. Once the drop is on every genuine pack, a pack without it that still says "paneer" or uses dairy imagery is going to stand out to shoppers, to competitors and to food safety officers.

> The logo is a packaging rule. The paneer draft is a naming rule. The space between them is where most of the enforcement risk will live, and that space is your copy.

## Where the word "paneer" is hiding in your marketing

When we audit a food brand's footprint, the pack is rarely the problem. The label went through a regulatory consultant. Everything written after launch did not. These are the places we would check first.

### Marketplace and quick commerce listings

Listing titles get stuffed with search terms. "Malai paneer cubes, soft, high protein" might sit on a product that is legally an analogue because sellers chase the search volume of the word "paneer." Under the draft, that title is marketing an analogue as paneer. The same goes for backend search keywords you control in seller panels, and for variant names that marketplaces auto-generate from the parent listing.

If you run listings across several platforms, pull a full catalogue export and search for "paneer", "malai", "dairy" and "milk" against every SKU whose licence category is analogue. It is a one-afternoon job and it will find things. Our [e-commerce team](/industries/ecommerce) does this as a standing check before any big sale event.

### Delivery app menus and cloud kitchens

FSSAI's earlier proposal in April 2025 went further than the current draft on food service. It suggested restaurants and caterers flag analogue use on menus and promotional material by putting "non-dairy" or "analogue" before the dairy term, with "analogue of paneer" in a kadhai paneer as its own example. That proposal also suggested packaged analogues be sold only in pre-packed units of at least 500 grams.

We cannot tell you those food service lines will be in the final rule. But a cloud kitchen brand writing "Paneer Butter Masala" over a dish made with an analogue block is carrying two risks at once: the draft's restriction on representation, and the consumer law exposure for misleading description that exists today. Menu names are copy. Treat them that way.

### Performance creative and creator briefs

Search ads, Meta ads and creator scripts are where "protein-rich paneer" lines get written fast and approved faster. If an analogue product's ad says paneer, it fails the draft on day one of the final rule. If a creator calls it paneer in a voiceover, the brand still owns that representation. We covered the evidence side of this in our post on [keeping a proof file for absolute claims](/blog/100-percent-claims-ccpa-fssai-proof-file), and the same discipline applies here: the brief has to tell the creator what the product may and may not be called.

### Recipe content and SEO pages

A plant-based or analogue brand that ranks for "paneer recipes" has probably built that traffic on pages that call the product paneer. Those pages are marketing. They will need renaming, and the redirects need planning now so you do not lose the traffic later.

## What to do this week, and what can wait

There are three groups affected, and the to-do list differs for each.

**Genuine dairy brands.** You are the beneficiary, but you have work. Plan the milk-drop logo into the next pack refresh rather than a panic reprint in mid-2028. Check every pack size against the table above, because the smallest SKUs (100 sq cm and below) are where a 6 mm square fights with everything else on the panel. Then decide whether you want to use the drop in ads and creator content once it is live. Expect competitors to do it, and expect the claim "real milk paneer" to get much louder.

**Analogue and plant-based brands.** Start the naming work now. You need a product name that does not lean on "paneer", a descriptor that is accurate (the April 2025 proposal floated "analogue of" and "non-dairy" constructions), and a plan for every listing, menu, ad and recipe page that uses the old word. If you have a view on the draft, file it before the window closes around 22 November. A brand that sells a clearly labelled, honestly positioned plant-based block has a reasonable case to make, and the consultation is the place to make it.

**Restaurants, caterers and cloud kitchens.** Find out what your paneer actually is. Ask your supplier for the licence category. If it is an analogue, rename the dish now, before a rule forces you to and before a customer complaint does.

## The honest caveat

The paneer restriction is a draft, and FSSAI drafts do change between consultation and notification. Some of the April 2025 proposals on food service and pack sizes may never appear in a final rule. We would not reprint packaging on the strength of the draft alone.

What we would do is fix the copy, because it is cheap, it is fast, and much of it is already exposed under existing consumer protection law for misleading description. Renaming a listing costs nothing. Explaining to a food safety officer why your analogue was called paneer across 40 marketplace listings costs a lot more.
