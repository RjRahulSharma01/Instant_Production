Fixing the interface after you get the notice no longer saves you. The Central Consumer Protection Authority fined McAfee Rs 1 lakh on 20 May 2026 and PhysicsWallah Rs 5 lakh on 1 June 2026 for dark patterns. Both companies had already changed the offending screens by the time the orders were signed. Both were penalised anyway, and both were ordered to file a compliance report within 15 days.

If you write checkout copy, subscription prompts, or urgency labels for an Indian brand, these two orders are the most useful thing published this year. They tell you exactly which words the regulator reads as manipulation.

## What PhysicsWallah actually got fined for

A pre-ticked "Donate for PW Foundation" checkbox added Rs 10 to every transaction unless the user removed it. It ran from 14 February 2024 to 24 December 2025 and collected roughly Rs 2.47 crore from over 21.36 lakh users.

PW's defence was that 63.9% of users opted out, which it argued proved the choice was free and visible. The CCPA rejected that outright. Under Rule 4(9) of the Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020, consent requires explicit and affirmative action. A box the user has to untick is not consent no matter how many people manage to untick it.

The second finding is the one most D2C and edtech teams have not internalised. PW advertised courses as "free" but required a mobile number and email address to access them. The CCPA created test accounts, confirmed the course content was identical across all of them, and concluded the data served no personalisation purpose. Collecting it was therefore forced action, not onboarding.

> Visibility is not consent. A charge the user has to remove was never a charge the user chose.

## McAfee lost on two words

McAfee's renewal prompt gave users "Accept Risk" or "Renew Now". No neutral cancel. "Renew Now" sat on a prominent red background; "Accept Risk" was subdued grey. McAfee recorded 3,55,133 renewals across January to December 2025 while that interface was live.

The company argued "Accept Risk" was a factual description of what happens when antivirus protection lapses. The CCPA held that the wording manufactured fear rather than informed anyone, and that the visual asymmetry steered the decision regardless of the literal words. The order found four separate prohibited patterns in one prompt: confirm shaming, interface interference, trick question, and forced action.

McAfee also argued that zero consumer complaints in 2025 proved no harm. The authority's answer is worth pinning above your desk: dark patterns work by suppressing objection while steering behaviour, so silence cannot be read as consent.

## The 13 patterns, and the four that catch marketers

The Guidelines for Prevention and Regulation of Dark Patterns, 2023 were notified on 30 November 2023 under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019, and name 13 specified patterns. Most of the enforcement so far clusters in four of them, and all four are copy and creative decisions rather than engineering ones.

| Pattern | What it looks like in practice | Live example from enforcement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| False urgency | "Limited time deal" with no expiry stated, countdown timers that reset | MediaNama found unexpiring "Limited time deal" labels across Amazon's app on 9 April 2026 |
| Basket sneaking | Pre-ticked donation, insurance, or add-on at checkout | PW's Rs 10 pre-ticked donation, Rs 5 lakh penalty |
| Confirm shaming | Decline copy written as an admission of failure or danger | McAfee's "Accept Risk" |
| Forced action | Gating a "free" asset behind a phone number you do not need | PW's free courses requiring mobile and email |

Most clients we work with have at least two of these live right now and did not put them there deliberately. They arrive through a growth experiment that lifted conversion, gets adopted as the default, and never gets re-reviewed. The Rs 10 donation ran for 22 months.

## Why the small fines still matter

The penalties look trivial. PW's Rs 5 lakh is about 0.002% of its Rs 2,495.61 crore FY 2024-25 revenue. Section 21 of the Consumer Protection Act permits up to Rs 10 lakh for a first violation and up to Rs 50 lakh for each subsequent one, so even the ceiling is small relative to the money involved. A June 2026 Datum Intelligence study estimated Indian online marketplaces earn Rs 25,000-28,000 crore a year from these practices.

The exposure is not the fine. It is the sequence. PW's order came from a suo motu review, not a complaint. McAfee's came from a single advocate's representation filed on 1 September 2025. Both ended with a public order naming the company, describing the interface in detail, and directing it to stop the practice everywhere. For a brand mid-raise or pre-IPO, that document is the cost. Zepto's Rs 7 lakh order landed weeks before its IPO filing.

The self-declaration route is not shelter either. Twenty-six platforms filed declarations that their platforms were free of dark patterns; the Datum study notes eight of them were fined within months.

## What to change this week

Pull your checkout and your paid landing pages and check three things.

First, every pre-selected charge. If money is added by default and removed by the user, it fails, no matter how small or how charitable. Make it an opt-in.

Second, every decline button. Write the opt-out in the same weight, size, and colour treatment as the accept, and phrase it neutrally. "No thanks" survives. "Accept Risk", "Maybe later, stay unprotected", "I don't want to save money" do not.

Third, every "free" in your ad copy. If accessing the thing requires a phone number, it is gated, not free, and you now need to be able to explain what that data does for the user. The CCPA opened test accounts to check. Assume the next regulator will too.

The honest caveat: the guidelines still leave real ambiguity. How much visual asymmetry between a subscribe button and a "no thanks" link crosses the line is unresolved, and platform-driven seller promotions sit almost entirely outside the current framework. That ambiguity is not a defence. Both companies argued good faith, fixed their interfaces mid-proceeding, and were fined regardless. If you are running [performance campaigns](/services/performance-marketing) that lean on urgency labels or gated lead magnets, review the creative and the destination together — enforcement so far has not treated them as separate things. The same applies to [ecommerce](/industries/ecommerce) checkout flows where growth and legal rarely review the same screen.
