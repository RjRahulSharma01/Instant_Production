If you advertise GST registration, company incorporation, or any other filing service in India on Google, the carve-out for India in Google's government documents policy does not switch itself on. Google's own policy page says excluded advertisers "must still apply" for the exclusion. Skip the form and your ads can be disapproved even though you qualify.

That is the short version. The longer version has a wrinkle that most agency checklists miss, and it affects which box you tick.

## What the policy covers

The policy is called "Other restricted businesses: Government documents and services." It applies to ads that promote direct acquisition of a listed document or service. The list on Google's policy page includes regional identification numbers, business identifiers, passports, driver licences, name or address changes, birth or death certificates, vehicle owner registration and unclaimed tax rebates.

If your ad promotes one of those and you are not a certified government provider, Google adds a "Not a government website" disclosure to your Search ads. That disclosure runs across all Search ad formats. Anyone searching for a government service sees it next to your listing, and it does nothing for click-through rate on a "Register your company in 3 days" headline.

Google also says violations do not cause immediate suspension. It issues a warning "at least 7 days prior to any suspension of your account." Seven days is the minimum, not the typical gap, so do not plan around it.

## The India carve-out, and where it sits

In March 2026 Google updated the policy, posting the notice on 2 March with enforcement starting 17 March. The notice says advertisers running business identifier ads that target only India as a region do not need to qualify as a government or authorized provider. Industry coverage at the time, including [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/google-ads-updates-government-documents-policy-carving-out-india-for-business-identifiers/), read this as relief for GST registration and company incorporation advertisers.

Now the wrinkle. The main policy page, as we read it this week, describes India's exclusion differently. It lists India as excluded from the regional identification numbers category, with a ration card as the example. It does not mention PAN at all. The business identifiers examples on that page are the US EIN, the UK Company Registration Number and the Australian business number.

We cannot tell you which wording Google will apply to a given account. The two pages are not identical, and the only safe reading is the narrow one: treat the exclusion as something you apply for and have approved, not something you assume.

| Service you advertise | Likely policy category | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| GST registration, company incorporation | Business identifiers | Apply for the regional or business exclusion, India-only targeting |
| Ration card or similar regional ID help | Regional identification numbers | Apply for the regional exclusion |
| Passport or visa filing | Passports, travel authorizations | Different route, see below |
| Advice, templates, calculators only | Not in scope | Select the "not in scope" option |

## The October change does not help most Indian agencies

On 4 August 2026 Google announced a second update, with enforcement from 5 October 2026. It lets non-government "authorized providers" run these ads, but only if three tests are met. Your domain must be linked from an official, publicly accessible government website that explicitly names it as authorized. You may promote only the document or service that authorization covers. And your ads must target only the matching region.

Google is specific about what does not count: commercial contracts, business or trade licences, business registry entries, and articles or blog posts, even on government-owned sites. Being listed in a company registry, or having a government tender, is not authorization.

For most Indian filing intermediaries that is a high bar. It is why the regional or business exclusion matters more than the authorized provider route for GST and incorporation work. Passport and visa facilitators are the ones most exposed to the October rule.

## How the certification form is laid out

The application offers four options:

- I qualify as a government provider.
- I qualify as an authorized, non-government provider.
- I qualify for a regional or business exclusion.
- My ads should not be in scope, because I am not promoting government documents and services.

Pick the exclusion option for India-only business identifier ads. Pick "not in scope" only if your ads genuinely do not offer to obtain a government document. An ad that says "Get your GST number in 48 hours" offers to obtain one. A guide titled "How GST registration works" does not.

Third-party coverage of the October rule puts advertiser verification at 5 to 7 business days to show in the account, and certification confirmation at 10 to 12 business days after that. Those are reported figures rather than Google commitments, so we would not promise a client a date based on them. What they do mean is that a form submitted the week a campaign launches will not have cleared by launch.

> The policy does not ask whether you are legitimate. It asks whether you filed the form that says so.

## What we do before a filing client goes live

Most clients we work with in this space are CA firms, incorporation startups and compliance platforms. Their ads are honest and their documents are real. The failure is almost always procedural.

We run the same short list each time:

1. Decide which row of the table above each campaign falls into, before writing any ad copy.
2. Keep India-only geo-targeting on every campaign you want covered by the exclusion. A campaign that also targets the UAE or the UK for NRI customers sits outside it.
3. Split exposed campaigns into their own account structure, so a disapproval on incorporation ads does not drag down the tax-advisory campaigns.
4. Make sure the business name on the advertiser verification matches the name on the certification form.
5. Screenshot the policy page the day you apply. Google has changed the wording twice this year.

None of this needs a budget. It needs someone to own the form. If your filing campaigns are live and you cannot say which option was selected on the account, that is the first thing to check this week, and it is also the kind of audit our [performance marketing team](/services/performance-marketing) runs before scaling spend.

Our honest caveat: Google has not published a plain list of Indian document types and which category each falls in, and the two pages disagree on wording. If your ads are disapproved after you apply, appeal with the exact category you applied under and the page text you relied on. Do not guess at a new category and resubmit.
