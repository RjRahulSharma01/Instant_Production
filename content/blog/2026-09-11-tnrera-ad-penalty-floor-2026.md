Tamil Nadu's real estate regulator has published a rate card for non-compliant property advertisements. A circular dated 24 June 2026, in force since 1 July, splits advertisement violations into major and minor and attaches a minimum rupee figure to each, scaled by the cost of the project. Running an ad without the project's TNRERA registration number and Form-C QR code is a major violation. On a project under ₹100 crore that starts at ₹2,00,000. On a project of ₹100 crore or more it starts at ₹5,00,000.

The word doing the work there is "minimum". TNRERA has said it can still reach for the higher penalties available under the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 where circumstances warrant.

## The four-box grid

| Violation | Project cost | Minimum penalty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Major | ₹100 crore and above | ₹5,00,000 |
| Major | Below ₹100 crore | ₹2,00,000 |
| Minor | ₹100 crore and above | ₹1,00,000 |
| Minor | Below ₹100 crore | ₹50,000 |

Major violations are four: advertising an unregistered project, publishing without the registration number and Form-C QR code, displaying a QR code that is unreadable or points somewhere irrelevant, and comparing the project's prices against alleged "market prices" or against a competing project.

Minor violations are the near-misses. Carrying only the registration number or only the QR code instead of both. Leaving out the TNRERA website address. Leaving out the promoter's office address. Failing to state that a project is a "RERA Exempted Project" where that applies.

## The unreadable QR code is a creative problem, not a legal one

Two of the four major violations are things a legal review will never catch, because they only exist once the asset is rendered.

A QR code that scans fine in the layout file can fail after platform compression, after a 1080x1080 crop for a Meta placement, or after it has been laid over a photograph with no quiet zone around it. A QR code pointing at last quarter's Form-C, or at the wrong tower in a multi-phase launch, is "irrelevant" in exactly the way the circular describes. Neither shows up in a copy deck.

Most clients we work with in property still treat the compliance block as a legal sign-off on the words. The circular has moved a chunk of the exposure into production and trafficking, which is where nobody was looking.

> A compliance block that cannot be scanned is functionally the same as one that was never there, and it now carries the same minimum penalty.

Build the scan into the QA step. Export the final asset at delivery resolution, scan it on a phone at arm's length, and confirm the page it opens is the Form-C for the exact project and phase named in the ad.

## The comparison clause will catch more campaigns than the QR code

Comparing the project's price with "market prices" or with a competing project sits in the major column. That is a staple of the category. "Priced 12% below the OMR average." "Compare with anything else in Thoraipakkam." Performance creative leans on it because it converts, and because a number reads as a fact.

Under this circular it is not a claim that needs a substantiation footnote. It is a major violation regardless of whether the comparison is true, sitting alongside advertising an unregistered project. If that line is in your rotating headline pool for a Tamil Nadu project, pull it today rather than at the next review.

## What the ad has to carry, precisely

The penalty framework enforces requirements TNRERA set out in an order dated 1 July 2025, which amended Regulation 6 of the TNRERA (General) Regulations, 2018. Those are specific enough to be checked against a layout:

- **Print:** registration number with Form-C QR code and the Authority's website address, at the top right corner, font size no smaller than twelve. On a multi-page ad, repeated on every page.
- **Brochures and leaflets:** same block, same minimum font size of twelve.
- **Outdoor**, including hoardings, buses and bus shelters: website address and registration number at the top right, at a font size at least 50% of the font used for the project name.
- **Electronic media:** TV and radio must speak the registration number and website address slowly and clearly.
- **Social media**, named as Facebook, Twitter, Instagram and LinkedIn: registration number plus a link to the TNRERA website in all project-related communication.
- **Online property portals:** registration number with QR code and the Authority's website address.

The same order bars travel-time claims outright, on the reasoning that "10 minutes from the airport" varies with traffic and so cannot be verified. It bars vague catch-all disclaimers of the "terms and conditions apply" kind. It bars unverifiable amenity counts such as "100+ amenities", and requires that amenities named in an ad match what the agreement or brochure actually promises. Promoter name, office address and contact details have to appear, and the project location has to match the sanctioned plan.

We covered the national shape of these rules in [an earlier piece on what a real estate ad can show](/blog/rera-real-estate-ad-rules-2026). What Tamil Nadu has added is the invoice.

## Why the floor matters more than the ceiling

Section 61 of the RERA Act already allowed a penalty of up to 5% of the estimated cost of the project for contravening the Act's provisions, and Section 63 allows a per-day penalty for failing to comply with the Authority's orders, cumulatively up to the same 5%. Those numbers are larger than anything in the circular. They were also discretionary, which meant a developer could reasonably assume a missing QR code would end in a warning.

A prescribed minimum removes that assumption. The question for a promoter is no longer whether the regulator will act on a small omission, it is which of the four boxes the omission lands in. For a ₹300 crore township, a single non-compliant creative starts at ₹5,00,000 before anyone argues about severity.

The practical effect lands on agencies and channel partners as much as on developers. A channel partner boosting a listing from their own handle, a portal rendering a listing card, a regional broker running WhatsApp creatives: each of those is an advertisement, and each carries the block.

## What to do this week

Pull every live asset for your Tamil Nadu projects into one folder: paid social, portal listings, brochures, hoardings, channel partner creatives, the website landing page. Scan each QR code from the exported file, not the source. Check the destination resolves to the current Form-C for that specific project. Confirm the number, the QR and the website address all three appear, since two of three is a minor violation with a ₹50,000 floor.

Then check your headline bank for price comparisons and travel times, and check the amenity list in every brochure against the sanctioned plan.

One honest caveat: this circular binds Tamil Nadu. Other state authorities have their own directions, and the specifics differ. What travels is the direction of movement. Regulators across states have spent the last two years writing the rules and are now moving to pricing them, which changes the calculation for any developer or [real estate marketing team](/industries/real-estate) that has been treating the compliance block as a formatting afterthought. If you run [paid campaigns](/services/performance-marketing) across multiple states, the safe default is to build to the strictest state's spec and apply it everywhere, because maintaining per-state creative variants is where these omissions get introduced in the first place.
