From 1 January 2027, a bank must publish a list of its direct selling and direct marketing agents on its own website and update it within seven calendar days of any change. The Reserve Bank of India defines those agents by what they do, not by what the contract calls them. If your agency runs lead generation for a lender, you need to know before that date whether the lender's compliance team will put you on the list.

We have covered the consent and dark pattern parts of these rules already, in our piece on [the "No" consent default](/blog/rbi-nbfc-consent-default-no-marketing-flows). This one is about a different clause: who counts as an agent, and what follows from being one.

## The definition is about function, not paperwork

The rules sit in the Reserve Bank of India (Commercial Banks, Responsible Business Conduct) Second Amendment Directions, 2026, reference RBI/2026-27/115, issued on 15 June 2026. A parallel set was issued for NBFCs the same day. RBI had circulated drafts on 11 February 2026 and revised them after stakeholder feedback.

Paragraph 4(10B) defines a DSA or DMA as a non-employee entity or individual that sells or promotes the bank's products, whatever their contractual label. Paragraph 4(10C) adds sub-agents: individuals engaged by a DSA or DMA who deal with customers at the point of sale.

The words "or promotes" and "whatever their contractual label" do the work. A vendor agreement that calls you a "marketing service provider" or a "media partner" does not by itself take you outside the definition.

## What the definition does not say

We read the text as summarised in the primary notification and in commentary from Vinod Kothari Consultants. Neither names advertising agencies, affiliates or creators. That is a gap, not a safe harbour.

So there are two honest readings. A narrow one treats DSAs and DMAs as the people who contact customers and push products: call centres, field agents, telemarketing vendors. A wide one treats anyone paid to generate customer enquiries for a specific product as promoting it.

A lender's compliance officer, facing an audit, will lean wide. Nobody in a regulated entity gets praised for a narrow reading that the RBI later disagrees with. Plan for the wide one.

## What being on the list triggers

The bank's obligations flow down to you in practice. These are the paragraphs that change how an agency works:

- **Paragraph 85B:** the bank's policy must set eligibility, due diligence, training, audit and penalty rules for agents.
- **Paragraph 85C:** an up-to-date public list of DSAs and DMAs on the bank's website, refreshed within seven calendar days of a change.
- **Paragraph 85D:** required qualifications or certifications for staff and sub-agents who sell products.
- **Paragraph 85E:** agents on bank premises must be clearly identifiable as non-employees.
- **Paragraph 85F:** a Code of Conduct, signed undertakings from each agent, and the Code displayed on the bank's website.
- **Paragraph 85N:** no coercion or misleading customers, and customer contact only between 09:00 and 19:00 unless the customer asks otherwise.
- **Paragraph 85O:** no misleading customers about identity or posing as a bank employee.

For NBFCs, Vinod Kothari's analysis of the matching directions reaches the same shape. The policy must cover agents, the list is public, and the lender stays responsible for agents and sub-agents. Those NBFC provisions are numbered differently from the bank ones above, so check the NBFC text for your client's paragraph references.

## Where this lands on a media plan

Most of our fintech work is paid acquisition, and four pieces of it touch these paragraphs.

| Activity | Likely exposure under a wide reading |
|---|---|
| Lead forms feeding a lender's CRM | Agency is generating enquiries for a named product |
| Outbound calling or WhatsApp follow-up on leads | Customer contact, so 09:00 to 19:00 and identity rules apply |
| Creator content with an apply link | Creator may be a sub-agent if they handle customers |
| Brand awareness with no product or link | Weakest case for DMA status |

The calling row matters most. A rule about contact hours already bites on any lead-gen vendor that calls back at 8 pm because that is when the lead is warm. The lender, not the vendor, answers to the RBI for it.

The creator row is where we expect the most confusion. A creator who posts a loan offer and never speaks to a customer looks like promotion. A creator whose followers message them to apply looks like a point-of-sale conversation. The definition does not draw that line for you.

## What to ask your lender client now

You have under two months. These are the questions we are putting to every regulated client this week.

1. Has your board-approved policy named the categories of vendor you treat as DMAs, and does it include marketing agencies?
2. If we are listed on your website, what name and scope will appear, and who approves the wording?
3. Which of our staff handle customers or leads by phone, and what certification or training will you require from them?
4. Will you ask us to sign the Code of Conduct, and does it bind our subcontractors, including call-centre partners and creators?
5. What is your process when our status changes, given the seven-day refresh window?

Get the answers in writing. If a client says "you are not a DMA", ask them to put that in the vendor agreement, because the lender's own audit trail will outlive that conversation.

> A contract label will not protect you. The RBI defined the role by what you do.

## Changes you can make before January

Separate the functions. If you run creative and media only, with no customer contact and no data handling beyond platform lead forms, document that scope in the statement of work. If you also call or message leads, either move that to a vendor the lender already lists, or accept DMA status for that part and train for it.

Cap calling windows in your dialler at 09:00 to 19:00 now, whatever the client's status. Keep a record of creator briefs, and write into them that creators do not take customer details or answer product questions beyond the approved script. Our [performance marketing](/services/performance-marketing) team has started doing this on [fintech](/industries/fintech) accounts.

One caveat. We are an agency, not a law firm, and the RBI has not published an FAQ confirming whether media vendors fall under the definition. If a client's counsel has a formal view, that view should override ours. Where there is none, assume the wide reading and design to it.
