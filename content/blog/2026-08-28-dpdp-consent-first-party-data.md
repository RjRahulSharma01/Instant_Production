The customer list you uploaded to Meta last week is probably not defensible under the DPDP Rules. Not because the data is wrong, but because nobody can produce the record showing each person on it agreed to be marketed to for that specific purpose. India's consent manager framework opens on 13 November 2026 and full enforcement lands on 13 May 2027. That gives you roughly fourteen months to fix an audience layer most brands have never audited.

## What actually changed, and when

The Digital Personal Data Protection Rules were notified on 14 November 2025, setting an 18-month phased implementation window. Three dates matter to anyone running paid media in India.

| Date | What happens | Why a marketer cares |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 13 November 2026 | Consent Manager registration framework becomes operational | Consumers get one interface to withdraw consent across brands at once |
| Late 2026 | Soft-enforcement phase ends; the Data Protection Board shifts from guidance to supervision | Legacy data collected before DPDP needs valid notice and consent behind it |
| 13 May 2027 | Full enforcement and adjudicatory powers | Penalties up to Rs 250 crore per violation become live |

The Consent Manager piece is the one people underestimate. Today, withdrawing consent means hunting through a brand's account settings, which most people never do. From November 2026, it becomes a single interoperable interface. Consent that could previously be assumed by inertia now has an easy exit.

## Purpose limitation is the clause that breaks retargeting

Under the DPDP Act, consent must be free, specific, informed and unambiguous, and it attaches to a stated purpose. Data collected to fulfil an order cannot quietly become the seed for a lookalike audience. That is not a stricter reading; that is the plain reading.

Most Indian D2C and services brands we work with have exactly one consent checkbox at signup, worded something like "I agree to the Terms and Privacy Policy." One checkbox cannot carry order fulfilment, transactional SMS, promotional email, WhatsApp broadcast and third-party ad platform matching all at once. Under purpose limitation, that is five purposes and it needs five recorded decisions.

Section 9 goes further and prohibits targeted advertising directed at children, with verifiable parental consent required before processing a minor's data at all. If you run edtech, gaming, or kids' apparel, that is not a checkbox problem. It is an age-assurance architecture problem.

## The uncomfortable part: you are the Data Fiduciary

The legal duty sits with the brand, not the agency, not the DSP, not the CDP vendor. When you push a hashed email list into a platform for matching, the platform is processing on your instruction. If the underlying consent does not exist, the exposure is yours.

> The question stops being "how big is my audience" and starts being "how much of my audience can I evidence."

We ask every new performance client the same two numbers now. What share of your addressable audience can you reach without a third-party signal? And what share of your CRM has a timestamped, purpose-specific consent record attached? The gap between those two numbers is the part of your media plan that has an expiry date on it.

## Why the timing is worse than it looks

Indian marketers spent 2023 and 2024 waiting to see whether Google would kill third-party cookies in Chrome. In April 2025 Google decided to keep them, which a lot of teams read as a reprieve and used as a reason to defer the first-party data work.

It was not a reprieve. Safari and Firefox already block third-party cookies by default, and given a real choice, users decline tracking more often than not. The signal was going to erode regardless. The deferral simply means the erosion now collides with a statutory deadline instead of arriving on its own schedule.

There is also a structural split forming. Karan Taurani at Elara Capital, analysing the Rules for Storyboard18, made the point plainly: first-party data fiduciaries are structurally better placed than third-party ones. Platforms sitting on consent-linked purchase history are advantaged. Adtech intermediaries dependent on borrowed signal take the sharpest hit. If your acquisition engine runs mostly on rented audiences, your cost curve is about to look like the one we described for [fintech CAC](/blog/fintech-cac-india), for a different but related reason.

## What we would actually do in the next two quarters

Consent design is a conversion problem before it is a legal one. Treat it that way and the work gets easier to fund internally.

### Map every collection point, then rank by volume

Website forms, app onboarding, WhatsApp opt-ins, offline events, marketplace order data, quiz funnels, loyalty signups. Most brands find between eight and fifteen. Rank them by how much of your matched audience each one feeds. Fix the top three first; the long tail can wait.

### Rewrite the consent moment as a value exchange

Roughly nine in ten consumers will share personal information when the exchange is clear, per BCG's consumer research. A skincare brand's skin-type quiz gets you ingredient preference, concern, and routine frequency, volunteered deliberately, with consent that is specific and documented. That is better targeting data than any inferred behavioural segment, and it survives an audit. This is where [content strategy and performance](/services/performance-marketing) stop being separate budgets.

### Start measuring consent as a marketing KPI

Grant rate, withdrawal rate, and the reason attached to each withdrawal. Put them on the same dashboard as CTR and CAC. From November 2026, withdrawal becomes one tap through a consent manager, and a rising withdrawal rate will be the earliest signal that your frequency or your creative has crossed a line.

### Audit before you are asked to

Dabur commissioned a full audit of its data practices early, mapping every collection touchpoint and testing consent validity across them. That is a multi-quarter exercise, not a sprint. If you are a mid-market [e-commerce](/industries/ecommerce) brand selling largely through marketplaces and quick commerce, you hold far less first-party data than you think, and the build takes longer than the audit.

## The honest caveat

Nobody knows yet exactly how the Data Protection Board will interpret the boundary between a brand's obligation and a vendor's. The Rules place the duty on the Data Fiduciary but do not spell out how far you can rely on an adtech partner to obtain and prove consent on your behalf. That ambiguity will get resolved by the first few enforcement actions in 2027, not by guidance you can read today.

Which argues for building the consent record you control rather than the one you inherit. If you can show a timestamp, a purpose, a wording, and a withdrawal path for every person in your audience, the interpretation question stops being your problem. Start with your three highest-volume collection points this quarter. That is a real fortnight of work, and it is the part that will still matter in May 2027.
