Sale windows are where creator compliance fails fastest. In its FY26 report (April 2025 to March 2026), ASCI reported that 90% of the 31 influencer cases it reviewed around Black Friday were violations, and only 57% were modified without a fight. If your Diwali and Black Friday briefs are written the way your normal ones are, expect the same result.

## What ASCI actually reported

The FY26 numbers, as reported by trade press from ASCI's annual complaints report: 1,609 influencer ads processed, 97.3% needing modification, and 54% of influencer violations tied to prohibited categories such as offshore betting. That headline is mostly betting. The part that matters for a retail or D2C brand is the event-level data.

Around the iPhone 17 launch, ASCI reviewed 21 influencer posts and reported all of them as non-compliant, with most corrected after notification. Around Black Friday (November to December 2025), 90% of 31 influencer cases were violations. Personal care alone accounted for 639 cases overall, 90% of which needed changes.

One caveat on sourcing: outlets disagree on some secondary figures, such as how quickly flagged posts were fixed, so we only use numbers that appear consistently. We also could not find a public breakdown of what exactly went wrong in the Black Friday cases. What follows is our reading of where sale-window posts tend to fail, not a quote from ASCI.

## Why sale windows break the normal process

A normal campaign has a brief, a draft, a review and a post date. A sale campaign has a code, a countdown and a creator who wants to post at 9 pm tonight because the offer ends at midnight. The review step is the first thing dropped.

Three failure patterns show up for us every season:

- The label goes in late, or only in the caption. ASCI's guideline treats disclosures buried behind a "more" tap, in a hashtag pile or at the end of a video as likely to be missed.
- The offer claim is copied from the brand's own ad. "Flat 60% off" travels from the brand creative into the creator's script with no check on what the 60% is measured against.
- Urgency language outlives the offer. A reel that says "last 3 hours" is still live and still being served 3 days later.

## The disclosure rules that apply to a 20-second sale reel

ASCI's influencer guideline allows labels such as Ad, Sponsored, Collaboration, Partnership, Affiliate and Free gift, plus the platform tools (Instagram's Paid Partnership tag, YouTube's Includes Paid Promotion). The platform tool is meant to be used in addition to your own label, not instead of it.

For video, clause 1.2(e) sets the timing by length. A 15-second reel needs the label on screen for at least 3 seconds. A 30-second reel falls in the band where it must stay up for one third of the video, so 10 seconds. Stories with no text need the label placed on the visual itself.

Clause 1.5 puts responsibility on the advertiser as well as the creator. That matters in sale season because the brand usually supplies the code and the claim.

> If the brand wrote the offer line, the brand owns the offer line, whoever says it on camera.

## Check the price claim before the creator says it

From 1 January 2027, the Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) (Amendment) Rules, 2026 (notified 9 September 2026) require a reduced price to be shown against the lowest price in the prior 30 days, under Rule 4(13). Those rules bind e-commerce entities, not creators directly. But a creator reading "was 4,999, now 1,999" off your brief is repeating a price claim your platform will soon have to defend.

This Black Friday is still before that date, so the rule is not yet in force. We are asking clients to build the habit now anyway, because the 30-day price log you need in January is the same document you need to answer a complaint today. We cover the pricing side in more detail in [our post on prior-price discount claims](/blog/discount-claim-prior-price-rule-2027).

## A pre-flight table for the sale brief

| Item | What to lock before the creator shoots | Who signs off |
|---|---|---|
| Disclosure label | Wording, on-screen duration by video length, platform tag on | Brand marketing |
| Offer claim | The reference price and the date range it was valid | Brand finance or category lead |
| Product claim | Any result or timeline claim, especially in personal care | Brand legal |
| Offer end | A hard takedown or edit time for urgency posts | Agency |
| Evidence | Screenshot of the live post with label visible, saved same day | Agency |

The last row is the one most agencies skip. ASCI's non-compliant list, with status as of 5 October 2026, names parties that failed label requirements both at first instance and on later routine checks. A saved, time-stamped screenshot is what lets you show that a label was there when the post went live.

## What the penalty exposure looks like

ASCI itself is self-regulatory and does not fine. It investigates, publishes findings and asks for ads to be modified or withdrawn. Fines come from the Central Consumer Protection Authority under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019: up to Rs 10 lakh for a first violation and up to Rs 50 lakh for later ones, with possible endorser bans of one year, extendable to three. We are quoting those figures from a secondary summary, so check the Act's text with your counsel before you rely on them in a contract.

## What to do this week

Pull your sale-season creator list and re-issue the brief with the table above attached. Ask each creator to send a draft with the label visible, not a promise. For personal care and supplement products, strip every numeric result claim from the script unless you hold the test report. And set the takedown time in the brief itself, so nobody has to remember it at midnight.
