An education ad that says "500 students placed, highest package 24 LPA" is non-compliant with ASCI's education guidelines unless it also states how many students passed out of that class, ties the numbers to the latest completed academic year, and can substantiate every figure. The placement count alone is the defect. Most edtech and institute creative we review fails on the denominator before it fails on anything else.

This is the part of education advertising that gets less attention than refund promises or rank claims, and it is the part that sits inside almost every performance creative for a course, bootcamp or MBA programme.

## What the rule actually says

The ASCI Guidelines for Educational Institutions, Programmes and Platforms cover universities, colleges, schools, coaching classes and edtech platforms. They run to nine numbered rules on top of the general ASCI Code. Three of them decide whether a placement ad survives a complaint.

Rule 5(a) says an ad cannot make claims about the extent of the passing batch placed, the highest or average compensation of the students placed, or the marks and ranking of students passed out, unless the data comes from the latest completed academic year and is backed by evidence. Rule 5(e) says an ad that cites placed students must state the total number of students passing out from the placed class. Rule 3 says an ad cannot imply that enrolment will guarantee a rank, high marks or a job unless the advertiser can submit substantiation.

Put together, the rules mean that "placed" is never a standalone number. It is a fraction, and the ad has to show both halves.

## The numbers ASCI itself publishes

Education is a recurring name in ASCI's complaint data. In its Annual Complaints Report for 2022-23, classical education ranked second among violative categories, with 13.8% of the ads ASCI processed not adhering to its guidelines, according to ASCI's 30 May 2023 press release announcing the updated education rules.

The half-yearly report for April to September 2025 is quieter on volume but still telling. ASCI scrutinised 71 education ads in that period, and education ranked fifth among violative sectors, behind offshore and illegal betting, personal care, healthcare, and food and beverage. About 45% of the education ads found violative were withdrawn voluntarily after ASCI's intervention.

That last figure is the useful one for an agency. A large share of these ads are not fought, they are pulled, which means the cost to the advertiser is a dead creative and a gap in the flight, not a legal bill. The cost is real all the same, because the ad that gets pulled is usually the one that was converting.

## Why the highest package is the riskiest line

"Highest package" is the single most common figure in education creative, and it is the one rule 5(a) names directly. It is also the number that tells the reader least. One offer at 24 LPA in a batch of 400 is a true statement and a misleading impression.

ASCI does not ban the figure. It requires the same discipline as every other claim: latest completed academic year, evidence on file, and the context that lets a reader judge it. The practical way to meet that is to put the highest and the average side by side, and the placed count next to the passing count.

Here is how the same claim reads before and after.

| Element | Fails rule 5 | Passes rule 5 |
|---|---|---|
| Placed count | "500 placed" | "412 of 640 students in the 2025-26 batch placed" |
| Salary | "Highest package 24 LPA" | "Highest 24 LPA (1 student), average 6.8 LPA (412 students)" |
| Period | No year, or an older batch | Latest completed academic year, stated |
| Evidence | Not on file | Offer letters and batch roll on file with the advertiser |

The figures in the right-hand column are illustrative, but the structure is the point. Every number has a base and a year attached to it.

## The disclaimer is a size rule, not just a wording rule

Rule 3 requires the line "past record is no guarantee of future prospects" on ads that make outcome claims. The detail most creative teams miss is the size requirement: the disclaimer's font should not be smaller than the size of the claim being made.

A 6-point footer under a 60-point headline does not meet that. It is also the format almost every static and video ad uses, because it is the format that keeps the headline clean. If your placement number is the hero of the frame, the disclaimer needs to be as large as that number, which changes the layout.

> A disclaimer that is smaller than the claim it qualifies is a claim with a footnote, and ASCI's rule is written to stop exactly that.

For video, we treat it as a frame-level check: the disclaimer has to be on screen at the same size as the number for as long as the number is. ## The "100%" trap sits right next to it

Rule 4 bars numerical claims of 100% where the claim is abstract and not quantifiable. ASCI's own examples are "100% Placement/Job assistance" and "100% Job opportunities / Job oriented courses."

The reasoning is simple. "Assistance" and "opportunities" cannot be counted, so a percentage attached to them cannot be true or false. If you want a number, count something countable: interviews arranged, companies that visited, offers made. Then show the base.

Rule 5(b) is the third quiet one. If an ad states a competitive rank, it has to give the full name and date of the publication or medium that released the rankings. "India's No. 1" with no source fails on that alone. We covered the CCPA side of this in [our note on coaching claims that now carry a penalty](/blog/coaching-ad-claims-ccpa).

## What to change in your creative this week

Pull the five highest-spending education creatives and run one test on each: can a stranger, from the ad alone, work out what fraction of the batch was placed and in which year? If not, the ad is exposed under rule 5(e), whatever else is right about it.

Then fix in this order. Add the denominator and the year to every placement count. Replace any "highest package" line with highest and average together. Resize the disclaimer to match the claim. Replace any "100%" attached to assistance with a countable figure. Finally, keep a one-page evidence file per claim, with the batch roll and offer data behind it, so a response to ASCI takes a day and not a fortnight.

One honest caveat. ASCI's code is self-regulatory, and its education guidelines do not carry their own penalty schedule. The legal exposure sits with the Consumer Protection Act, 2019 and the authority enforcing it, and a complaint can travel to either.
