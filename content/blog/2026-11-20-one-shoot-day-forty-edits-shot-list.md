A shoot day that produces three ads was planned as a film, not as a library. The fix happens before anyone books a camera: write the shot list around hooks, formats and edit slots, frame every take for vertical and horizontal at once, and leave the set with raw material for thirty or forty cuts instead of one hero video and two trims.

This is the most common production problem we see with founders who come to us after a shoot "didn't work". The footage is usually fine. The plan was built for the wrong output.

## Why three assets is now a real problem

Paid social in India runs on creative volume. The ET BrandEquity and Ipsos State of Digital Marketing in India 2025 report puts digital ad spend at ₹49,000 crore in FY25, with social media at 29.8% and online video at 29% of that digital spend, each growing about 20% a year. The same report names ad fatigue and rising CAC among the hurdles marketers call critical.

Meta has also changed what it rewards. Its Andromeda retrieval system, which narrows millions of eligible ads to a few thousand candidates for each impression, pushed Meta's own guidance towards variety. As Jon Loomer summarises Meta's April 2025 messaging, "the focus has shifted from niche targeting to creative diversification as the best lever." He is equally clear that diversification is "not about pumping out near-duplicates with minor tweaks."

In competitive Indian categories, a creative typically fatigues in 21 to 35 days. Three or four active ad sets need somewhere between 15 and 30 assets a month to keep running. A brand that shoots three assets a quarter is not short of budget. It is short of footage.

## Where the other thirty-seven assets went

When we look at a shoot that yielded three ads, the cause is almost always one of these.

### The shot list followed the script

A single 60-second script gets shot top to bottom, in order, once. Every take serves one edit. Nobody captured the product in hand without dialogue, the reaction shot, the before state, or the founder saying the same line three different ways.

### Only one aspect ratio was protected

The camera was framed for 16:9 because that is what the brand film needed. Cropped to 9:16 later, heads get cut off and the product drifts out of frame. Spec guides compiled from Meta's placement requirements put Reels and Stories at 9:16 and feed at 4:5, with roughly the top 14% and bottom 35% of a Reels frame covered by interface. A horizontal-only take rarely survives that crop.

### There was no hook plan

The first three seconds decide whether a Reel is watched. If the shoot captured one opening, every edit starts the same way, and to the platform and the viewer they read as near-duplicates of the same ad.

### Nobody owned the edit slots

The editor received 400 GB of footage and a brief that said "make some cuts". Without a list of what each edit is for, you get the hero film and two shorter versions of it.

## How to plan the same day for forty edits

You can apply this yourself before your next shoot. It costs time at the planning stage and almost nothing on the day.

### 1. Start from the edit list, not the script

Write down the edits you want to exist at the end, then work backwards to shots. A useful split for one product or service:

| Edit type | Count | What it needs from the shoot |
|---|---|---|
| Hook variants on the main ad | 8 to 10 | Ten different opening lines or visuals, same body |
| Problem and solution cuts | 6 | Before state, the struggle, the product in use |
| Founder or expert to camera | 6 | Three to four talking points, each delivered twice |
| Product B-roll and demos | 8 | Hands, close-ups, texture, unboxing, results on screen |
| Objection answers | 4 | One line each on price, trust, time, comparison |
| Static and carousel frames | 6 to 8 | Clean stills pulled from video at high shutter speed |

That table adds up to roughly forty. It is the same people, the same location and the same day.

### 2. Shoot hooks as a separate block

Give the hooks their own hour. Same framing, ten different first lines, delivered at full energy each time. This is the cheapest creative testing you will ever do, because the body of the ad stays constant and you can isolate what actually stops the scroll.

### 3. Frame for both ratios in the same take

Shoot wide enough for 16:9 with the subject placed so a centred 9:16 crop still holds the face and the product, clear of the top and bottom interface zones. If you have two cameras, lock one vertical. Mark the safe zones on the monitor. This one habit is the single biggest reason a planned shoot day yields around forty usable edits rather than five.

### 4. Capture silent coverage

At least a third of your edits will run without sound or with music only. Shoot product moments with no dialogue over them, and get on-screen text space into the frame deliberately.

### 5. Brief the editor with slots, not vibes

Hand over the edit table above with a file name convention for every slot: product, angle, hook number, ratio, version. Versioned, named exports are what let you read results later and know which hook won.

### 6. Build a hook bank from the results

Once the cuts are live, record which openings held attention and which died. That list becomes the brief for the next shoot. Over three or four cycles, you stop guessing.

> A shoot day is not a cost per video. It is a cost per tested idea, and that number should keep falling.

## The honest caveats

Forty edits is not forty winners. Loomer's own tests of 25 and 50 ads per ad set found no clear advantage from the larger number on its own, and "quality still beats quantity." Volume matters only because it gives you more genuinely different ideas to test.

The plan also has limits. A single location and one presenter will not give you creative diversity across personas or settings. If your category depends on showing different customers, you need creators or a second set-up. And if your ad account spends under about ₹2 lakh a month, forty assets is more than the budget can test properly; plan fifteen strong ones instead.
