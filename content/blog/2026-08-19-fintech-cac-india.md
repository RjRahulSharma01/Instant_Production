Fintech acquisition costs in India are rising for a reason most teams are not budgeting for. It is not just auction competition. Lenders have become far more selective about who they will underwrite, so the same ad spend now buys fewer approvable applicants — and from January 2027, the regulated entity is answerable for what its influencers and affiliates say. Cheap volume channels are getting more expensive to use and riskier to keep.

The number that made this concrete: in Q1 2026, average cost per lead for a mid-market Indian lending app on Google Search [crossed ₹1,400, up 34% from eighteen months earlier](https://maliktimes.in/cac-rising-indian-fintech-apps-2026-2/). Nothing about the creative got worse over that period. The definition of a good lead got narrower.

## Rising CAC and a deteriorating ratio are different problems

Worth separating these, because the fixes are different.

CAC rising while lifetime value rises with it is a growth problem — annoying, manageable, mostly a question of payback period. CAC rising while LTV stays flat or falls is a business model problem, and in consumer lending the CAC-to-LTV ratio has deteriorated meaningfully for most players.

The cause is upstream of marketing. After the RBI's interventions on digital lending, lenders tightened underwriting. The pool of applicants who both want the product and will be approved for it got smaller. Marketing is paying full price for traffic and converting a smaller share of it into anything that generates revenue.

If your CPL is up 34% and your approval rate is down, your real CAC has moved considerably more than 34%. Most dashboards do not show this, because they stop at the lead.

## The 2027 rules change who carries the risk

On 11 February 2026 the RBI floated amendments to its advertising framework and invited industry feedback. [The framework comes into effect on 1 January 2027](https://www.storyboard18.com/influencer-marketing/explained-rbis-new-rules-for-financial-advertising-and-influencer-marketing-ws-l-101236.htm), and the substantive change is one sentence: if a financial product is being marketed — by a bank employee, an influencer, an affiliate or a fintech partner — the regulated entity is ultimately responsible for the message that reaches the consumer.

Read that again with an affiliate network in mind. Every publisher running your offer, every finfluencer paid on a CPA basis, every lead aggregator reselling the same form to four lenders, becomes your compliance surface.

Separately, SEBI has proposed an advertising code for investment products that would prohibit guaranteed-return claims, exaggerated claims, testimonials, and unfair comparison with competing products. [Rewards-based acquisition](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/investment/story/investment-apps-may-have-to-abandon-rewards-based-customer-acquisition-under-sebis-proposed-ad-code-539240-2026-06-26) — refer a friend, get free stocks — sits awkwardly under that draft.

If a meaningful share of your acquisition comes from testimonial-led creator content or reward mechanics, you have about four months to find out whether that survives.

## What the disclosure requirements already demand

None of this is new in principle. RBI's September 2022 guidelines already mandate that digital lending advertisements disclose the name of the regulated entity, and lending ads must carry the NBFC or bank name, the RBI registration number, and transparent interest rate information.

The number of live campaigns that quietly do not is remarkable. Usually because the disclosure was on the landing page and someone shortened the ad copy, or because an affiliate built their own creative and never sent it for review.

| What breaks most often | Where it usually happens |
|---|---|
| Missing regulated-entity name | Ad copy shortened for character limits |
| No RBI registration number | Affiliate-built creatives, never reviewed |
| Interest rate stated as "starting from" only | Performance creative testing variants |
| Undisclosed paid partnership | Finfluencer content, especially Instagram |
| Guaranteed or implied return | Investment app creator briefs |

> If you cannot produce, today, every live creative running under your brand including your affiliates', you do not have a compliance position. You have an assumption.

## Where the cost actually comes down

Three things move the number, in order of how much they move it.

**Qualify earlier and accept a smaller top of funnel.** If underwriting will reject 70% of applicants from a channel, stop paying to send them. Pre-qualification questions on the landing page reduce lead volume and improve real CAC, and most teams resist this because the lead-count graph goes down. Look at approved-customer cost instead, and the decision is obvious within a fortnight.

**Move budget from prospecting to owned demand.** Content and organic search cost more up front and less per acquisition over time, which matters most exactly when paid CAC is inflating. This is slow, which is why teams under quarterly pressure keep not doing it, and why the ones who started two years ago now have a structural cost advantage.

**Fix creative volume, not creative quality.** Financial services creative fatigues like everything else, and in a restricted category you have fewer levers to pull, so cadence carries more of the load. If you are shipping four assets a month against a dozen live ad sets, fatigue is your CPM problem. [The creative testing loop](/blog/creative-testing-loop) sets out the arithmetic.

What does not work: bidding harder on the same audience, or chasing a cheaper channel that turns out to be cheaper because its leads do not convert.

## The creator question

Finfluencer content works in India. It also carries the most concentrated risk under the incoming rules, because the message is drafted by someone outside your compliance process and published without review.

The workable version is narrow: creators on a fixed fee rather than CPA, with copy approved before publication, disclosure in the first line rather than the fourth hashtag, and a written agreement that they will not make return claims. That is slower and more expensive per post than the market rate, and it is the only version that survives January.

If you are running creator content into paid amplification, [turning influencer content into paid](/blog/influencer-content-into-paid) covers the rights and whitelisting side, which is a separate contract most brands get wrong.

## The data layer everyone forgets

Three frameworks shape fintech advertising in India, and teams usually plan for two. RBI covers lending and banking ads, SEBI covers investment products, and the Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 covers what happens to the personal data you collect through the landing page attached to those ads.

The DPDP obligations are the ones quietly ignored, because they sit with whoever built the form rather than with whoever bought the media. Consent has to be specific and informed rather than a pre-ticked box. The purpose has to be stated at collection. A person can ask for their data to be erased, and somebody has to actually be able to do that across your CRM, your lead aggregator, your calling vendor and your remarketing audiences.

That last point is where it gets uncomfortable. Most acquisition stacks in Indian fintech push lead data to four or five downstream systems within seconds of submission. If you cannot enumerate those systems, you cannot honour an erasure request, and you do not know where your customers' financial data is sitting.

None of this shows up in a CAC calculation. It shows up later, as a very expensive quarter.

## What to do before January

Inventory every live creative including affiliate and creator content. Most teams cannot do this in a day, which is itself the finding.

Decide now which acquisition mechanics you are prepared to defend to a regulator, and start moving budget off the ones you are not. Doing this in December, under a deadline, with a quarterly target outstanding, will produce worse decisions.

And instrument approved customers rather than leads, so that when CPL rises you can tell whether it matters.
