On 1 October 2026, the free-form replies your team sends inside WhatsApp's 24-hour customer service window stop being free. Utility templates sent inside that same open window lose their waiver on the same date. Meta announced this on 10 August 2026, and if your brand runs any meaningful volume of inbound WhatsApp conversations, your November invoice will not look like your September one.

The rate itself is small. The structure of the charge is what changes your numbers.

## What exactly flips on 1 October

Two things that were free become billable, and one thing that was already free stays free.

Service messages — a reply that is free-form, sent inside an open 24-hour window, and not generated by Meta's own AI — have been free since November 2024. From 1 October they are charged per delivered message, at the same per-message rate Meta already applies to utility and authentication templates in that market.

Utility templates sent inside an open service window were charged before July 2025, free from July 2025 onward, and go back to being charged from 1 October 2026. Authentication templates were always charged in-window and are unaffected.

| Message type | Inside the 24-hour window | Status from 1 Oct 2026 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Service message (free-form reply) | Free since Nov 2024 | Charged per message |
| Utility template | Free since Jul 2025 | Charged per message |
| Authentication template | Always charged | No change |
| Marketing template | Always charged | No change |
| Inbound message from customer | Free | Still free |

The 72-hour free entry-point window for conversations that begin with a Click-to-WhatsApp ad or a Facebook CTA button stays in place. So does volume-based discounting on utility and authentication templates — but service messages get no volume tier at all. The per-message rate is flat however many you send.

## The India number

Utility and authentication templates in India currently sit at roughly ₹0.115 per message. Marketing templates went up about 10% at the start of 2026, from ₹0.7846 to ₹0.8631. Meta committed to publishing the final per-country October rates by 1 September 2026, and service messages are expected to land at the utility rate.

Then add 18% GST — Meta's messaging charges are treated as imported OIDAR services, and your BSP's platform fee is taxed too. If you are GST-registered you can claim input credit; if you are not, that 18% is real cost.

So the honest arithmetic looks like this. A support conversation where an agent sends six free-text replies costs ₹0 today. From October it costs roughly ₹0.69 before GST, ₹0.81 after. Run 5,000 such conversations a month and a line item that read zero becomes about ₹40,000 a year in messages you were already sending.

That is not catastrophic. What makes it worth attention is that the cost now scales with how badly your conversations are designed.

## Chattiness is now a line item

Under the old model, message count inside the window was free, so nobody optimised it. Agents split thoughts across three bubbles because that is how people text. Bots sent "Got it", then "One moment", then the answer. Flows fired a confirmation and then a follow-up nudge. None of it cost anything.

From October, every one of those bubbles has a price. The brands that will see the ugliest jump are exactly the ones that thought they had solved support: high-volume automated flows, chatbots that acknowledge before answering, and utility templates fired inside active conversations.

> The cost of a WhatsApp conversation is now a function of how many turns it takes to resolve — which means message design just became a P&L question, not a UX preference.

Most clients we work with have never counted replies per resolved conversation. It is not in any dashboard by default. Meta's WhatsApp Manager does break volume down by category — service, utility, marketing — so you can establish a September baseline and measure the October delta against it. That is the single most useful thing to do this month, and it takes an afternoon.

## The four moves worth making before October

**Count your turns first.** Pull September's service-message volume per business phone number from WhatsApp Manager. Divide by resolved conversations. If your average is above four replies per conversation, that is where your money is going.

**Consolidate replies.** One structured message with the order status, the expected date and the next step beats four fragments. This is a copywriting job more than a technology one — rewrite your top twenty canned responses as single, complete messages.

**Move data capture into WhatsApp Flows.** Collecting a name, a pincode and a preference through three back-and-forth messages now costs three messages. One interactive Flow step costs one.

**Audit your template categories.** Meta can reclassify a template from utility to marketing, and marketing costs about 7.5× more in India. Any utility template carrying promotional language is a reclassification risk. Read them all before October, and strip the soft-sell lines out of your order-confirmation copy.

There is a fifth move that matters for anyone running paid: route more first-touch demand through Click-to-WhatsApp. Those conversations keep the free 72-hour entry-point window, which is now materially more valuable than it was in September. If you are already spending on [performance marketing](/services/performance-marketing) and sending traffic to a landing page that ends in a WhatsApp button, the CTWA objective gets you the same conversation with a longer free window attached.

## The AI wrinkle

Separately, from 1 August 2026 Meta bills replies generated by its own Meta Business Agent per token, at $2.00 per million tokens globally — roughly 4–5 cents per message on typical exchanges. A third-party AI bot does not get that treatment: its replies are service messages, so you pay your AI vendor and Meta's per-message delivery charge on top.

That is a genuinely awkward incentive. For high-volume, simple deflection, Meta's own agent may end up cheaper than the stack you already built. For anything that needs your product catalogue, your returns policy or your CRM, it will not be. Worth modelling both rather than assuming.

## What we would actually do

If WhatsApp is a support channel for you, treat October as a deadline for a message-design pass, not a vendor conversation. Your BSP cannot negotiate Meta's base rate — it is the same ₹0.8631 marketing rate across Gupshup, Interakt, AiSensy and everyone else, with only the markup differing. Switching providers saves paise; resolving in three replies instead of six saves half the bill.

The honest caveat: Meta's final India rates were due by 1 September 2026, so confirm the published figure with your BSP before you build a budget on ₹0.115. And if your WhatsApp volume is mostly outbound marketing broadcasts rather than inbound support, this change barely touches you — spend your energy on the [unit economics that actually move](/blog/d2c-unit-economics-2026) instead.

For [D2C and e-commerce brands](/industries/ecommerce) running WhatsApp as a real support desk, the work is small and the deadline is fixed. Do it in September.
