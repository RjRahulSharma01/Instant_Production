A "Coming Soon" banner for a project that is not yet RERA-registered is an advertisement, and Bihar RERA has fined a developer Rs 1 lakh for exactly that. The order, passed on 24 December 2025, means a teaser creative, an expression-of-interest form or a site hoarding all need a registration number behind them before they go live.

For performance teams, that changes when a real estate lead-gen campaign is allowed to start. The pre-launch "build the list" phase is the risky one.

## What Bihar RERA actually decided

The case was a suo motu proceeding against PRU-RLDA Projects Pvt. Ltd. (the show cause notice had gone to M/s Alphabet Infra Pvt. Ltd., which the developer said was only a brand name) over "One World, Brahmapura Railway Colony" in Muzaffarpur. Inquiry Commissioner Sanjaya Kumar Singh found that a pamphlet inviting bookings, an online notice and "Coming Soon" posters on the site all fell within the definition of "advertisement" in Section 2(b) of the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016.

Section 3(1) bars a promoter from advertising, marketing, booking or offering a project for sale before it is registered. The Authority held the material breached that section and imposed Rs 1 lakh under Section 59(1), plus a direction to register immediately.

The developer's defences all failed:

- The posters were only "for identification".
- A registration application was pending.
- The online material had been taken down.
- "Alphabet Infra" was a brand, not the promoter.
- It had apologised.

The Authority treated the brand-versus-promoter point as immaterial. Liability turns on whether a promoter or its agency advertised.

## Why Rs 1 lakh is the wrong number to look at

Rs 1 lakh was described as a moderate penalty. The ceiling is different. Section 59(1) allows a penalty of up to 10% of the estimated cost of the project, and the base is the whole project value, not the units sold. On a Rs 200 crore project, that is Rs 20 crore of theoretical exposure for a poster.

The order also warned of consequences under Section 59(2) for continued non-compliance, with payment due within 60 days. We would not plan around the small figure. It reflects the facts of this case, not a safe harbour.

| Material | Typical pre-launch use | Bihar RERA position |
|---|---|---|
| Site "Coming Soon" board | Brand recall, walk-in leads | Advertisement (Section 2(b)) |
| Pamphlet inviting bookings | Local lead capture | Advertisement, breach of Section 3(1) |
| Online notice or teaser post | Retargeting audience seeding | Advertisement |
| Apology or takedown afterwards | Damage control | Does not cure the contravention |

## What this does to a pre-launch media plan

Most clients we work with in real estate run a teaser phase: a landing page, a Meta lead form, "register your interest" copy, sometimes a hoarding near the site. The logic is sound commercially, because early lists convert cheaply. Under this reading of Section 3(1), none of it is exempt just because no unit has been priced or booked.

The practical consequences are narrow but real:

- **Registration number first.** If the RERA registration is not issued, the teaser phase does not start. The cheap list-building window moves to after registration, not before.
- **Interest forms count.** A form that invites people to express interest in a named project reads as soliciting, which is the point of Section 2(b). We would treat it the same as a booking enquiry.
- **Agencies sit in the chain.** The reasoning in the order is that liability follows whoever advertised, promoter or agency. Our brief template now asks for the registration number and the registering authority's name before we publish anything for a project.
- **Takedown is not a defence.** Pulling the creative after notice reduced nothing here, and it will not undo a screenshot in the Authority's file.

> A pending application is not a registration, and a teaser is not a placeholder. If the number is not on the creative, the creative is not ready.

## The adjacent rule that still applies after registration

Once registered, a different failure appears. MahaRERA's Nagpur Bench fined a promoter Rs 10,000 under Section 11(2) because the registration number and QR code in a Lokmat newspaper ad (29 March 2025) were blurred and the Authority's website address was missing. The developer's argument that the ad was small was rejected. We covered the format side of this in our note on [MahaRERA's font-size fine](/blog/maharera-ad-font-size-fine) and the state-level penalty floors in [the Tamil Nadu RERA piece](/blog/tnrera-ad-penalty-floor-2026).

So there are two gates: no advertising before registration, and legible disclosures after it. Teams usually plan for the second and forget the first.

## What to do before the next launch

Build the launch calendar backward from the registration date, not from the construction milestone. Put the RERA number and the issuing state authority in the creative brief as mandatory fields, and make the ad ops checklist block publishing without them.

For list-building before registration, the honest option is non-project content: brand, location and builder track record, with no project name, price, configuration or enquiry prompt. Whether even that is safe depends on the state authority and the specific wording, so ask the developer's legal counsel to sign off on the exact copy. This is not legal advice, and one Bihar order is not binding on other state authorities, though it shows how a regulator reads the definition.
