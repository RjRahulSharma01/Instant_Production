Google published a policy note on 3 September 2026 saying that from 30 October, advertisers can run personalised ads for alcohol on YouTube inventory wherever local law permits it. The same note names four countries where this is not available: Egypt, India, Indonesia and Poland. So if you handle a beverage alcohol brand in India, nothing in your media plan changes next month.

The reason that carve-out exists is worth understanding, because it is not a Google decision that might soften next quarter. It is a reflection of the fact that India does not permit the underlying advertisement at all, which means the targeting question never arises.

## What the policy actually says

The update sits in Google's [personalized advertising policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/143465) and applies to YouTube inventory. It opens up alcohol, alcohol-related products and alcohol alternative beverages as categories that can be personalised against, in markets where that is legal.

Two limits survive the change everywhere, including the markets that are now open. Targeting based on health information related to alcohol stays prohibited under Google's Health sensitive interest category. And ads are not personalised for minors at all, regardless of category.

The India exclusion is written as "not yet available with this update," which reads like a sequencing note. Treat it as the current state rather than a promise, because the local blocker is a legal one and Google does not control it.

## The Indian blocker is the CCPA guidelines, not the platform

Direct advertising of liquor has been restricted in India for decades. What sharpened the picture was the Guidelines for Prevention of Misleading Advertisements and Endorsements for Misleading Advertisements, notified by the Central Consumer Protection Authority on 9 June 2022 under Section 18 of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019, and brought into force immediately.

Those guidelines did something the older rules had not: they defined surrogate advertising in law. An advertisement counts as surrogate if it indicates or suggests, directly or indirectly, that it is an advertisement for goods whose advertising is prohibited or restricted, or if it borrows the brand name, logo, colour, layout or presentation associated with those goods.

There is a proviso. Using a brand name that also appears on a restricted product is not automatically surrogate advertising, as long as the ad is not otherwise objectionable under the guidelines. That proviso is where music CDs, soda, playing cards and packaged drinking water live. It is also thinner than most brand teams assume, and it reads alongside Rule 7(2)(viii) of the Cable Television Network Rules, 1994.

> The guidelines bind the advertiser, the agency and the endorser separately. One non-compliant asset creates three exposures, not one.

Penalties come from the parent Act, not the guidelines: the CCPA can impose fines of ₹10 lakh, rising to ₹50 lakh for repeat contraventions.

## Where this bites in practice

Most clients we work with in the beverage category do not think of themselves as running alcohol ads. They run ads for a brand extension: a soda, an event property, a music platform, a glassware line. The media buy is for the extension. The creative, in an honest reading, often is not.

| What you are buying | Available in India | Binding constraint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Personalised alcohol ads on YouTube | No | Local law, per Google's own note |
| Brand extension ads on any platform | Yes | CCPA guidelines, Clause on surrogate advertising |
| Creator content featuring the parent brand | No | Endorser liability under the same guidelines |
| Organic brand handles and owned channels | Yes, with care | Same surrogate test applies to all media |

There is a planning consequence hiding in that table. Because the restricted parent brand cannot be personalised against anywhere in your Indian media, the extension carries the entire targeting job on its own merits. That is a real constraint on audience building: you are optimising for people who want the soda or the music property, not for people who drink the parent brand, and the two audiences overlap far less than a portfolio deck usually suggests. Budget the extension as its own product, with its own cost per acquisition, or the numbers will not hold up at review.

The row that catches people out is the third one. The 2022 guidelines apply to all advertisements regardless of form, format or medium, and they explicitly reach endorsers. A creator posting a paid reel that visually echoes a liquor brand is inside the definition, and so is the agency that briefed it.

## What to do with this before 30 October

If you run a portfolio that includes restricted categories, the useful work this month is an audit rather than a media plan. We look at three things every time a beverage brief comes in.

First, does the extension product genuinely exist and sell, with distribution you can point to? A product that exists only as an ad is the clearest surrogate signal there is.

Second, does the creative stand on its own without borrowing the parent brand's visual furniture? Colour, bottle silhouette, tagline cadence and even the sound design get tested against the borrowed-presentation limb of the definition.

Third, has every creator and celebrity in the campaign been briefed in writing on what they cannot show or say? Their exposure is personal, and they will hold you responsible for the brief you gave them. Our [influencer marketing](/services/influencer-marketing) briefs for restricted categories carry a separate annexure for exactly this.

The honest caveat: none of this is settled. The proviso on brand extensions is vague enough that two competent lawyers will read the same ad differently, and CCPA enforcement in this category has moved in bursts rather than steadily. What is settled is that platform availability is not your compliance test. Google closing the door on personalised alcohol ads in India is a symptom of the rule, not the rule itself, and a campaign that is legal here will still be legal on 30 October when nothing on YouTube changes for you.

If the brief on your desk is for a brand extension and you are not sure which side of the line it sits on, that is the conversation to have before the shoot, not after the [media plan](/services/performance-marketing) is signed off.
