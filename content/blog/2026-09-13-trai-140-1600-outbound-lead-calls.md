If your team calls a lead who filled a form yesterday, that call is a promotional call. It belongs on a 140xx number, it requires registration with a telecom service provider under the TCCCPR framework, and a customer who has blocked your sector on the DND registry will never see it ring. The 1600xx series, the one that arrives looking trustworthy, is not open to you unless you are an entity regulated by the RBI, SEBI or PFRDA calling somebody who is already your customer.

Most brands we talk to have these two series mixed up, usually because a dialler vendor sold them "TRAI compliant calling" without saying which half of the framework they were buying.

## The two series do genuinely different jobs

TRAI issued a clarification on 10 July 2026 because media coverage had blurred the line badly enough that the regulator felt it had to restate the position in a press note. The split is simple once you see it written down.

| | 1600xx | 140xx |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Service and transactional calls | Promotional calls |
| Who can use it | RBI, SEBI, IRDAI and PFRDA regulated entities, plus government bodies for government-to-citizen communication | Entities across all sectors |
| Who you may call | Your existing customers | Anyone who has not blocked your sector on DND |
| Can apps tag it | No | No |
| Can it be blocked | No | Yes, where the customer has registered a DND preference for that sector |

The asymmetry in that last row is the whole point. A 1600 call cannot be tagged, blocked or filtered at all under the Telecom Commercial Communications Customer Preference Regulations. A 140 call cannot be tagged or filtered either, but it can be blocked outright, and the person doing the blocking is the consumer, through a preference they registered on the DND registry or the TRAI DND app.

## Who actually gets a 1600 number

TRAI's Direction of 19 November 2025 set hard dates. Commercial banks, including public sector, private sector and foreign banks, had to be onboarded by 1 January 2026. Large NBFCs with asset size above ₹5,000 crore, payments banks and small finance banks followed on 1 February 2026. The remaining NBFCs, co-operative banks and regional rural banks came in on 1 March 2026.

On the SEBI side, mutual funds and asset management companies were due by 15 February 2026 and qualified stockbrokers by 15 March 2026. Other SEBI-registered intermediaries may migrate voluntarily after their registration details are verified. PFRDA's central recordkeeping agencies and pension fund managers were also due on 15 February 2026. The insurance sector was left out of that Direction, with TRAI saying the timeline was still under discussion with IRDAI.

At the time the Direction was issued, about 485 entities had already adopted the series across more than 2,800 numbers. That is the entire eligible universe you are competing against for a consumer's attention on an incoming call, and it is a small, closed list.

> If you are not on that list, no amount of dialler configuration buys you the unblockable slot.

## What this changes if you buy leads and call them

Three things break quietly.

The first is your connect rate assumption. A lead who submitted a form has given you consent in the contract sense, but DND operates at the sector level and independently of your form. If they blocked your sector on the registry, a 140 call does not reach them. Your CRM will log it as unanswered, and you will keep paying for more leads to compensate for a funnel leak that no amount of budget fixes.

The second is what you report as cost per qualified lead. When we rebuild funnel maths for a lender or a broker, the number that moves most is not cost per lead, it is the share of leads that were ever reachable by phone in the first place. The [fintech CAC piece](/blog/fintech-cac-india) covers why the reachable subset matters more than the headline figure.

The third is your fallback. Plenty of teams respond to poor 140 connect rates by quietly dialling from ordinary 10-digit mobile numbers, on the theory that an unmarked number gets picked up more often. It does, for a few weeks. Then the number accumulates spam reports, the call management apps tag it, and you burn through SIMs. That is a treadmill, not a strategy, and it sits outside the registration framework TRAI expects promotional callers to operate inside.

## The tagging fight is the part worth watching

The clarification did not arrive in a vacuum. TRAI has been pushing to expand its enforcement reach over third-party call management applications, and in July 2026 it sought designation as an authorised agency under the Information Technology Act so it could act against platforms including Truecaller, Hiya and Whoscall. Its complaint is that these apps tag or block calls originating from the designated 1600 and 140 series, which defeats the purpose of the series and pushes legitimate callers back to ordinary numbers.

The Internet and Mobile Association of India has pushed back on the proposed TCCCPR amendments, arguing that requiring call management apps to report user spam complaints to telecom operators and barring them from tagging designated series calls goes beyond TRAI's statutory remit, and that forcing OTT platforms to share proprietary data with operators conflicts with the IT Act and the DPDP Act.

However that resolves, the direction of travel is clear. The trust label on an incoming call is becoming a regulated asset rather than a crowdsourced one. Whether your call arrives looking legitimate will increasingly be decided by which numbering series you are entitled to, not by how many people have or have not reported you.

## What we change in the funnel

For clients who cannot get a 1600 number, which is most of them, we stop treating the phone as the primary first touch and rebuild the sequence around channels where reachability is not sector-gated. WhatsApp and email carry the qualification load, and the call becomes the second or third contact, placed after the lead has already replied somewhere. Connect rates on a warmed lead are a different number entirely.

We also separate the two call types in the CRM from day one. A service call to an existing customer and a prospecting call to a purchased lead are different regulatory objects even when the same agent makes both from the same desk. Teams that log them in one bucket cannot tell whether a poor connect rate is a DND problem, a data quality problem or a script problem, and they end up fixing the wrong one.

If you are running outbound lead follow-up in [fintech](/industries/fintech), education, insurance or real estate, the honest next step is unglamorous: pull your last 90 days of outbound dials, split them by originating number series, and check what share went out from plain 10-digit numbers. In most audits we run, that share is well over half. Everything downstream of a fix starts there, and our [performance marketing team](/services/performance-marketing) treats it as a media problem rather than a telecom one, because the money leaks in the media plan.

One caveat worth stating plainly. The insurance deadline is still pending as of this writing, and TRAI's proposed TCCCPR amendments are unresolved. Anyone selling you a permanent answer on outbound calling compliance right now is selling you a snapshot.
