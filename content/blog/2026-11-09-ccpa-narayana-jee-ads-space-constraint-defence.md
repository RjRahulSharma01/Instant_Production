"We did not have the space" is no longer a defence for leaving course details out of a results ad. On 11 June 2026 the Central Consumer Protection Authority fined Narayana Educational Institutions Rs 8 lakh over its JEE Advanced 2024 ads, and it called the omission deliberate. The bench pointed out that the same ads ran on a website and social pages where there is no page to run out of.

If you produce results creative for a coaching brand or an edtech, this order changes what a compliant ranker post looks like.

## What the CCPA actually found

The case is *In Re: Misleading Advertisement by Narayana Educational Institutions with respect to JEE Advanced 2024 Results* (CCPA-2/38/2024-CCPA). The authority acted on its own motion, with no complaint filed. It reviewed ads on Narayana's website, its social media and national print, including The Times of India. The bench was Chief Commissioner Nidhi Khare and Commissioner Anupam Mishra.

The ads carried the names, photos and ranks of successful candidates. They did not clearly say which course each candidate had taken. The CCPA's concern was that a parent reading the ad would assume every face was a full-time classroom student, when the candidates had come through different routes: classroom programmes, distance learning, or shorter courses.

Two further defects sat on top of that. The "AIR 1" claim did not say it was a category rank rather than an open-category rank. And where course details did appear, they were set in a font the order treated as too small for an average reader to notice.

## The space-constraint argument, and why it died

Narayana's defence was simple. There was no room in the creative to disclose course details.

The CCPA rejected it for two reasons. In print, it said the space went to promotional content instead of mandatory disclosures, which is a choice. Online, where scrolling removes layout limits, the ads carried no disclosures at all. That second point is the one most performance teams should sit with. A regulator does not have to argue about newspaper column inches when the same campaign appears on a landing page with unlimited space and the same omission.

The authority also turned down the claims that the omissions were an unintended side effect of design, and that disclaimers did appear somewhere inside the creatives. Having a disclaimer in the file is not the test. Whether an ordinary consumer can read it is the test.

> A disclosure you had room for and chose not to print is a decision, and the regulator will describe it as one.

## What the order tells you to do

The directions are specific enough to copy into a creative checklist.

| Direction in the order | What it means for your ranker creative |
| --- | --- |
| Pull the flagged ads from all platforms | Takedown covers every placement, not only the one the notice mentions |
| Disclaimers with the same prominence, legibility and font scaling as the student's name or rank | The course line sits at the size and contrast of the rank, not in a footer |
| Compliance report within 15 days | Keep an audit trail of what ran where, before you are asked |
| Truthful and complete disclosure in future ads | Complete means course, mode and year, not a generic "T&C apply" |

The parity rule is the practical one. If the rank is 72-point bold on a gold ribbon, a 6-point "course details on website" line does not match it. Design the disclosure into the layout from the first draft instead of squeezing it in at the end.

## Category rank is its own claim

"AIR 1" is read by parents as the best result in the country. If that rank belongs to a reserved or restricted category, the ad needs to say so beside the rank, in the same breath. The Narayana order treats the missing category label as a separate source of confusion, not a rounding error.

This is easy to get wrong in a template. A designer builds a rank card with a big number and a name field. The category sits in a database column that never makes it onto the card. Add it as a required field in the template so the card cannot render without it.

We covered the May 2026 Motion Education and CLC Sikar penalties in [our note on ranker photos and the three disclosures](/blog/coaching-ranker-ads-ccpa-motion-clc). Those orders dealt with consent and claim wording. The Narayana order adds the course-type question and, more usefully, a ruling on how much weight a "no space" excuse carries.

## A working checklist for results creative

Most clients we work with run results ads in bursts after an exam result lands, with a small design team and a tight turnaround. That is exactly when the course line gets cut. Build the checks in before the rush.

1. Every featured candidate has a recorded course: programme name, mode (classroom, distance or test series) and year.
2. The course line appears on the creative at the visual weight of the rank, on every placement including stories and display.
3. Category rank is labelled wherever a rank is shown.
4. Your landing page repeats the disclosure in full. It has the space.
5. Enrolment records and consent forms for each featured student are complete and signed. The order also noted unsigned enrolment forms and incomplete records in the material Narayana submitted.
6. One person owns a pre-publish check, and the log shows who approved what.

If you run these campaigns through [performance marketing](/services/performance-marketing), put the disclosure in the ad template and the landing page template together, so a media buyer cannot launch one without the other. For the wider set of rules on [education advertising](/industries/education), the three orders above are a good starting set to brief your design team on.

## The honest caveat

The reporting we could access does not give the statutory sections the CCPA relied on, and we have worked from news summaries of the order rather than the full text. Read the order itself before you rewrite a template around it. Rs 8 lakh is also small next to a results-season media budget. The cost sits in the public order, the 15-day compliance report, and the pulled campaign in the weeks you most need it running.
