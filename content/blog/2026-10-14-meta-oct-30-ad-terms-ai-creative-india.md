From 30 October 2026, Meta's rules for its generative AI ad tools stop being a separate document and become part of the Self-Serve Ad Terms, the agreement that governs your whole ad account. Whoever accepts the terms on behalf of the account accepts the AI rules too. That makes AI-made creative an account-level obligation, not a tool setting.

## What Meta has actually said

Meta has named four documents in this update: the Commercial Terms, the Product Catalog Terms, the Self-Serve Ad Terms and the Ad Creative Generative AI Terms. The only change Meta has spelled out is the last one. In its words, the Self-Serve Ad Terms "are being updated to incorporate the Ad Creative Generative AI Terms."

We could not find a published change summary for the Commercial or Catalog Terms, so we are not going to guess at those. Read the new text when it goes live. The wording below comes from a careful third-party reading of the current documents, not from the final legal text, and it is worth checking against Meta's pages on the day.

## Why folding it into the account terms matters

A standalone tool document is something a designer clicks through once. The Self-Serve Ad Terms are the document your finance head, your agency and your client's legal team all point to when something goes wrong. Under those terms Meta can reject or remove any ad, and the advertiser stays solely responsible for ad content, targeting and placements.

So if a generated image shows a claim you cannot substantiate, or a synthetic person who looks like someone real, the tool does not share the blame. The account holder carries it. That was always the practical position, but now it sits in the document that counts.

## The four points we would check first

Based on the current wording, these are the places an agency's workflow is most likely to rub against the terms:

| Area | What the current terms say | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Ownership of outputs | You keep rights in your inputs. Meta keeps rights in AI outputs and lets you use them on its platforms. | Whether a generated asset is reused on a website, a TV cut or a client's offline media |
| Personal data in prompts | Do not put personal information into inputs. | Prompts that paste customer names, lead lists or testimonials |
| AI info label | Meta applies an "AI info" label to ads made or significantly edited with its tools. | Your creative approvals, because the label appears whether or not the brand wanted it |
| Catalog content | Product Catalog Terms are also being updated. | Titles, prices and images feeding Advantage+ catalog ads |

The ownership line is the one we expect to cause arguments. If your contract with a client says they own every deliverable outright, an asset generated inside Meta's tool may not let you promise that. Say so in the scope of work before the client discovers it.

## The India layer sits on top, not inside, Meta's terms

Meta's label and India's label are two different things. The Information Technology (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) amendment notified by MeitY on 10 February 2026, in force from 20 February, brought "synthetically generated information" into the due-diligence rules. It covers audio, visual and audio-visual content created or altered so that it appears real, and excludes routine editing and formatting.

For platforms, the rules require prominent labelling, embedded provenance metadata and a user declaration before posting, checked by automated tools. A platform that knowingly lets violations through risks its safe harbour. That is a platform duty, and it is the reason platforms are rewriting their own ad terms. It does not automatically make a platform's label enough for you. We covered that gap for Google in [Your Google AI ad label doesn't satisfy India's SGI rule](/blog/google-ads-ai-label-sgi-gap), and the same logic applies here.

> A platform label tells viewers what the platform detected. It does not tell a regulator what you decided.

One practical wrinkle: detection of third-party AI content leans on metadata such as C2PA. Resizing and exporting a video can strip that metadata, so a clip you generated elsewhere may reach Meta looking unlabelled, and may be labelled or not depending on the export path. If your pipeline resizes after generation, test it rather than assume.

## What we are doing with clients before 30 October

Most clients we work with run a mix of agency-made and tool-made assets, often in one carousel. The fix is boring and quick:

1. Tag every asset in the creative tracker as human-made, Meta-AI-assisted or third-party-AI, with the tool name.
2. Strip customer names and lead data out of any prompt template.
3. Ask the account admin to accept the new terms deliberately and check Ads Manager for pending notices, instead of letting someone click through at 11 pm.
4. Add one sentence to client contracts about AI-generated asset ownership and reuse outside Meta.
5. Keep your own disclosure on AI-generated people and voices, in line with our note on [labelling AI video ads in India](/blog/ai-video-ad-labelling-india), whatever label Meta adds.

If AI video is a real share of your output, this is also the moment to decide which tool makes which asset. Our [video production](/services/video-production) work is split exactly this way, so a client can see which frames came from a camera and which from a model.

## Caveats

We have not seen Meta say that ads will stop delivering if the terms go unaccepted, and we would not plan around a deadline Meta has not stated. Several details in this post rest on a secondary read of the existing terms, and the final text may differ. Treat 30 October as the day to read the real document, not the day to trust ours.
