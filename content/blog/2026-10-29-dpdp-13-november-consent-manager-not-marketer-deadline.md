On 13 November 2026, one thing under India's DPDP Rules starts: the registration of Consent Managers. The notice, consent, retention and breach duties that actually touch your lead forms and retargeting audiences start on 13 May 2027. If your team has a "DPDP go-live" ticket on the November sprint, it is aimed at the wrong date.

That does not mean you can switch off. It means the next six months are a build window, and the way you spend it will decide how much of your lead data survives May.

## What actually commences, and when

The Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025 were notified on 13 November 2025 as G.S.R. 846(E), alongside G.S.R. 843(E) for the parts of the Act itself. They commence in three steps, not one.

| Date | What starts |
|---|---|
| 13 November 2025 | Rules 1 and 2, plus the Data Protection Board's procedural rules (17 to 21) |
| 13 November 2026 | Rule 4 (Consent Manager registration) and sections 6(9) and 27(1)(d) of the Act |
| 13 May 2027 | Rules 3, 5 to 16, 22 and 23, Act sections 3 to 17, and the penalty provisions |

Some trackers list the November date as 14 November. The Gazette-based summaries we checked say 13 November. Either way, the point stands.

Rule 4 lets an applicant apply to the Board for registration as a Consent Manager. The applicant must be a company incorporated in India with a net worth of at least ₹2 crore, and the platform must be independently certified against standards the Board publishes. That is a regulated intermediary, not a brand running ads.

## Why the confusion exists

Two things muddy this. First, plenty of compliance blogs write "13 November 2026" in a heading and bury the distinction in paragraph six. Second, MeitY floated a shorter runway in January 2026. At a stakeholder meeting on 22 January, it proposed cutting the 18-month window to 12 months, which would have moved the main date to 13 November 2026.

Reports differ on whether that cut was meant for every Data Fiduciary or only Significant Data Fiduciaries. Changing the date needs an amendment to Rule 1 in the Gazette. As of 8 October 2026, none had been published, so the date in law is still 13 May 2027.

Treat the proposal as a warning about direction, not as a date. If you are a large consumer brand, a lender, or an edtech with sensitive audiences, the chance that you are classed as a Significant Data Fiduciary is not trivial, and those duties (a data protection officer, an independent auditor, an impact assessment every 12 months) are the ones MeitY wanted sooner.

> A deadline you have not been given is still a deadline you can plan against. A deadline you have misread is just a surprise on a different day.

## What a Consent Manager changes for a performance team

Most of our clients will never register as a Consent Manager. But once the platforms exist, an individual can grant, review and withdraw consent across many companies through one interface. That changes three things on the media side.

**Withdrawal becomes cheap for the user.** Today, opting out of a brand's WhatsApp marketing means finding a link or replying STOP. Through a Consent Manager it becomes one tap, and your systems are expected to act on it. A CRM that syncs audiences to Meta and Google once a week is slow for that job.

**Consent becomes purpose-specific.** Rule 3 requires a notice that says what data is collected and why, in plain language, in English by default and in the 22 Eighth Schedule languages on request. A bundled "I agree to terms and promotions" tick is a weak record against that notice.

**Records matter more than banners.** The question in a dispute is not whether you showed a cookie banner. It is whether you can produce, for one phone number, the notice version it saw, the purpose it agreed to and the timestamp.

## The six months, used properly

We would do five things before May, in this order.

1. Inventory every collection point that feeds paid media: landing page forms, lead ads, WhatsApp opt-ins, IVR scripts, offline event sheets. Teams almost always undercount, usually because of old campaign microsites nobody owns.
2. Separate the service consent from the marketing consent in the form itself, with a distinct purpose line for ad audience use.
3. Store the notice version and a timestamp against every lead, not only the checkbox value.
4. Build a suppression path that reaches your Customer Match, custom audience and email lists within days of a withdrawal, not at the next monthly refresh.
5. Check processor contracts with your CRM, dialer and WhatsApp provider. Rule 6 safeguards and breach reporting will sit across them.

Our earlier notes on [first-party data and consent](/blog/dpdp-consent-first-party-data) and on [child data and retargeting](/blog/dpdp-child-retargeting-ban-edtech) cover items two and four in more detail. If your lead engine runs on [performance marketing](/services/performance-marketing), the consent record is now part of the funnel, not a legal appendix to it.

## The honest caveat

The Board itself was not fully functioning as of the latest reporting we could confirm. MeitY invited applications for the Chairperson and Members on 6 May 2026, and as of 1 August 2026 no appointments had been reported by LiveLaw. So even the November registration window has no body ready to process applications. Do not read that as slack. Read it as the reason to finish your own records before anyone asks for them.
