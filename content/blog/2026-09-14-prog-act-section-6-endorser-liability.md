Section 6 of the Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025 does not say "no gaming company shall advertise." It says no person shall make, cause to be made, aid, abet, induce, or otherwise be involved in the making of any advertisement that directly or indirectly promotes an online money game. Read that list again. The creator who films the spot, the agency that briefs it and the publisher who sells the slot all sit inside it.

That is the part most brand teams have not absorbed. The Act got covered as a gaming story, and the gaming companies were the ones who lost a business. But the drafting is deliberately wide, and the penalty under Section 9(2) runs to two years of imprisonment or a fine of up to fifty lakh rupees, or both. A second conviction carries a floor: minimum two years and minimum fifty lakh rupees, rising to three years and one crore.

## What the Act actually prohibits

The Act received Presidential assent on 22 August 2025 as Act No. 32 of 2025. Section 5 prohibits offering an online money game. Section 7 prohibits banks and payment facilitators from moving money towards one. Section 6 is the advertising provision, and it stands apart from the other two in a way that matters.

Section 2(1)(g) defines an online money game as any game played by paying fees, depositing money or staking anything else in expectation of winning money or other enrichment, whether the game is based on skill, chance or both. The old skill-versus-chance argument that kept rummy and fantasy platforms alive through a decade of state litigation is switched off at the definition stage. E-sports are carved out. Everything with a stake is not.

"Other stakes" is defined at Section 2(1)(j) as anything convertible to money, including credits, coins and tokens, real or virtual, bought directly or indirectly. So a title that takes no cash but sells tokens which cash out is inside the definition, and an ad for it is inside Section 6.

## Why the definition of "advertisement" is the trap

Section 2(1)(a) does not define advertisement at all. It borrows the definition from the Consumer Protection Act, 2019, which reads: any audio or visual publicity, representation, endorsement or pronouncement made by means of light, sound, smoke, gas, print, electronic media, internet or website, and includes any notice, circular, label, wrapper, invoice or such other document.

Endorsement is in the definition itself. The CPA also defines endorsement separately at Section 2(18) to cover a verbal statement, a demonstration, or the depiction of a person's name, signature or likeness in a way that makes a consumer believe it reflects that person's own opinion or experience.

> A creator reading a script they did not write is still making a representation, and the Act does not care whose idea it was.

Put the two together and an unboxing-style reel, a "use my code" story, a jersey sponsorship frame, a podcast read and an affiliate link in a bio all qualify as advertisement. Section 6 then adds "directly or indirectly," which catches the surrogate route: promote the parent brand, promote the "free to play" sibling app that shares the name, promote the news portal that exists to funnel traffic.

## Where the agency sits

This is the bit we keep raising with clients running [influencer programmes](/services/influencer-marketing) that touch sport, fantasy and creator commerce. Section 11 makes an offence by a company attributable to every person who at the time was in charge of and responsible for the conduct of that part of the business. The only escape in Section 11(2) is proving the offence happened without your knowledge, or that you exercised all due diligence to prevent it.

That turns your brand-safety process into a legal document. Due diligence is not a claim you make afterwards; it is a file you produce. Independent and non-executive directors who are not in the actual decision-making are expressly protected under the proviso to Section 11(3). A campaign manager who approved the media plan is not.

One asymmetry is worth knowing. Section 10 makes offences under Section 5 and Section 7 cognizable and non-bailable. Section 6 is not on that list, which means an advertising offence is treated differently at the arrest stage. That is a procedural difference, not an exemption, and it is a thin thing to build a risk position on.

## What changed on 1 May 2026

The Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Rules, 2026 came into force on 1 May 2026 and set up the Online Gaming Authority of India as an attached office of MeitY, chaired by an Additional Secretary, with Joint Secretary level representation from Home Affairs, Finance, Information and Broadcasting, Youth Affairs and Sports, and Law and Justice.

The practical piece for media planning is the determination mechanism. The Authority classifies a specific game as an online money game or a permissible category, acting on its own motion, on application by the provider, or on notification by the Central Government. It weighs payment of stakes, expectation of monetary winnings, the revenue model, and whether in-game assets are monetised outside the game. It aims to finish within 90 days, and records the result in a determination order specific to that game and that provider.

Specific to that game and that provider. A publisher-level clearance means nothing. If a client runs a portfolio of titles, each one needs its own answer.

## The platforms moved first

Google announced on 7 January 2026 that it would disallow all rummy and daily fantasy sports promotions targeting India, effective 21 January 2026. It gave fourteen days, against its usual thirty to sixty for a policy change of that size, and cited compliance with local legal requirements without naming them.

The certification pathway that previously let licensed daily fantasy operators run in India was removed rather than tightened. No licence now buys access. The prohibition applies across Google's advertising products, including Display and Video 360 and programmatic buys of Google inventory, and it triggers on any campaign whose geo targeting includes India.

| Route | Status after 21 January 2026 |
| --- | --- |
| Google Ads with DFS certification | Pathway removed, no certification accepted |
| Programmatic buy of Google inventory | Same prohibition applies |
| Campaign targeting India plus other markets | Blocked on the India inclusion alone |
| Creator content with a referral code | Outside platform policy, inside Section 6 |

That last row is the one that gets missed. Platform enforcement removes a buying channel. It does not remove statutory exposure, and it does not reach organic creator posts at all. A brand can be fully compliant with Google's policy and still be inside Section 6 through a creator deal it signed six months ago that is still live on a profile grid.

## What we actually do about it

Three things, in order.

First, an inventory sweep. Every live creator contract, every affiliate arrangement, every sponsorship frame, every bio link. Not just what is running in [paid media](/services/performance-marketing) now, but what is still publicly visible from earlier flights. Section 6 attaches to the advertisement, and an old reel sitting on a profile is still an advertisement.

Second, a per-title check rather than a per-partner one. Ask whether the specific game takes a stake, including token purchases that convert, and ask whether there is a determination order. Absence of a ban is not the same as a determination.

Third, a written approval trail on every gaming-adjacent brief: who reviewed it, against what, on what date. That file is the only form Section 11(2) due diligence takes in practice.

The honest caveat: the Rules commenced in May 2026 and the determination process runs to 90 days per game, so the published picture of what is and is not an online money game is still filling in. If a title has no determination order yet, the safe reading is that the classification question is open and your exposure is open with it. Nobody has been prosecuted under Section 6 for an influencer post yet, which is a fact about how new the law is, not a fact about how it reads.
