SEBI has notified its final Common Advertisement Code, and it changes how any broker, mutual fund, investment adviser or research analyst can work with creators. Celebrity ads, which include influencers above a follower threshold and computer-generated virtual influencers, need prior approval from the entity's supervisory body. Endorsements can promote the brand, not a product's performance. The rules apply from roughly April 2027.

## What was notified, and when

The code sits in the SEBI (Intermediaries) (Second Amendment) Regulations, 2026, notification SEBI/LAD-NRO/GN/2026/323, dated 8 October 2026. It inserts a new Chapter IIID, Regulations 16G to 16Q, into the SEBI (Intermediaries) Regulations, 2008.

It takes effect on the 180th day after Gazette publication. MediaNama reports publication on 9 October, which puts the start date in early April 2027. One trade report we read gave 8 October as the effective date. The notification text says 180 days, so we are planning against April.

Scope covers stock brokers, depository participants, online bond platforms, investment advisers, research analysts, mutual funds, AMCs and portfolio managers. It replaces the separate ad codes those entities followed before.

## Who now counts as a celebrity

Regulation 16G(2) lists the categories. The one that matters for most agencies is the third: influencers above a follower threshold set by the Advertising Standards Council of India, which ASCI can revise later.

The draft had hard-coded five lakh followers. The final text points to ASCI instead. ASCI's own celebrity definition, introduced in 2023, uses 500,000 followers on a single handle or Rs 40 lakh a year in campaign income. SEBI's code does not restate a number, so check the current ASCI figure when you brief.

| Category | Draft | Final code |
|---|---|---|
| Influencer threshold | 5 lakh followers | Whatever ASCI sets, revisable |
| Virtual influencers | Covered | Covered, listed as their own category |
| Founders and senior management in own ads | Treated as celebrities | Excluded |
| Post-issuance reporting | Within 24 hours | Within 3 working days |
| Approval and review deadline | Unspecified | 4 working days |

Other categories include lead actors, top 50 names in a national celebrity index, international sportspersons, long-running TV anchors, and winners of prominent competitive shows. There is also a catch-all for anyone SEBI or a supervisory body thinks can influence viewers.

## What changes in a typical finfluencer deal

Most briefs we see for broker and AMC campaigns ask a creator to explain a product and show the returns. That brief does not survive the code.

> A creator can say they trust the brand. They cannot say what the product earned.

Under Regulation 16K, a celebrity can endorse at the brand or entity level. A product can be named, but no inducement and no product-specific claim can ride with it. Regulation 16O separately bans testimonials, guaranteed or risk-free return language, implied repeat of past performance, and vague adjectives such as "high returns".

Three practical consequences follow:

- Scripts that quote a fund's return, even with a disclaimer, are out of bounds for celebrity-tier creators.
- Any creator above the ASCI threshold needs the ad approved before it goes live, not reported afterwards.
- A virtual influencer or AI avatar is treated like a human celebrity, so the same approval and brand-level rule applies.

Costs matter too. The entity cannot pass celebrity campaign costs to clients or charge them to mutual fund schemes. If your retainer model bundles creator fees into a fund's expense line, that needs a rethink before April.

## The approval clock is your new production deadline

The supervisory body has four working days to decide on celebrity ads. For brokers and online bond platforms that body is the stock exchange; for mutual funds it is AMFI; for portfolio managers it is APMI. Investment advisers and research analysts have their own supervisory bodies.

Four working days is fast for a regulator, but it is slow for a content calendar. A creator who posts on trend cycles will not wait for a reel to clear. We would build a standing buffer of at least a week between the final cut and the go-live date for any SEBI-regulated client, and stop promising same-week turnaround on celebrity-tier content.

Non-celebrity ads do not need prior approval. They must be uploaded to the supervisory body's portal within three working days of issue and reviewed within four. The reporting format will come in a separate SEBI circular, which had not been issued when we read the notification.

## Rules that apply to every ad, creators or not

These matter for performance teams as much as influencer teams, because they cover paid social and in-app creative:

- Every ad must carry the entity name, SEBI registration number, logo and prescribed disclaimers. For SMS and push notifications, a link to those details on the official site is acceptable.
- Named securities used as examples need the "quoted as an example and not as a recommendation" line on the same frame, not buried at the end.
- Charts and statistics need a source and a date or period.
- Vouchers, coupons or any incentive to trade, open an account or download an app are barred.
- Dark patterns, as listed in Annexure I of the CCPA's 2023 guidelines, are prohibited.
- Ratings can only come from a Past Risk and Return Verification Agency.

There is also a clause that gets little attention. If a third party runs an unauthorised ad for the entity, the entity must act within seven days, including legal action, inform its supervisory body and disclose the incident on its website. That covers affiliate and lookalike creators.

## What the code leaves open

MediaNama's reading of the final text flags gaps worth watching. SEBI declined to resolve overlap with CCPA jurisdiction, so a single ad could still draw action from two regulators. AI ads may also need labelling under India's IT Rules, which brings MeitY into the picture. The code says nothing on AI-generated investment advice or on crypto and prediction market ads.

Founders with large followings are exempt from the celebrity tag in their own ads. That creates a gap: a founder-led reel avoids the approval an outside creator would need. We would not build strategy around that loophole, because a supervisory body can still treat anyone it considers influential as a celebrity.

## What we would do before April 2027

Audit every live creator contract for return figures, testimonial language and product-level claims, and rewrite scripts to brand-level messaging. Check each creator's follower count against the ASCI threshold now, and again when ASCI revises it. Move celebrity-tier content onto an approval-first calendar, and add a clause that lets the client pull a post within seven days of an unauthorised copy appearing.
